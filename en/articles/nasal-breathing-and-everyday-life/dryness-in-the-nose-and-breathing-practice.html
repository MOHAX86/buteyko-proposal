Dryness in the nose and breathing practice

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Dryness in the nose may depend on the indoor air, irritants, the condition of the mucous membrane and the way a person breathes. If it is persistent or pronounced, the cause needs to be found, rather than trying to overcome it with a breathing load.

Slight dryness in the nose may occur during the heating season, when air conditioning is running, on contact with dust or after a long stay in a room with dry air. Sometimes it increases during breathing practice.

The causes vary, so dryness cannot automatically be considered either a sign that an exercise is being done incorrectly or a necessary part of restoring nasal breathing.

Why the mucous membrane needs moisture

The nasal mucosa warms and humidifies inhaled air. The mucus layer also helps trap particles, and the movement of the cilia helps remove them.

When the mucous membrane dries out, the following may appear:

  • burning;
  • itching;
  • a feeling of tightness;
  • crusting;
  • congestion;
  • soreness;
  • minor bleeding.

Because of the unpleasant sensations, a person begins to draw in air more often, to sniff or to switch to breathing through the mouth.

Possible causes of dryness

The condition of the mucous membrane is affected by:

  • low air humidity;
  • high room temperature;
  • dust and smoke;
  • chemical irritants;
  • allergic and inflammatory processes;
  • a recent infection;
  • some medicines;
  • frequent use of vasoconstrictor drops;
  • overly intensive rinsing;
  • increased volume and speed of airflow.

With deep and frequent breathing through the nose, more air passes over the mucous membrane, which can increase the evaporation of moisture. But this is only one of the possible causes.

Dryness during Buteyko practice

Properly selected practice should not cause rough, noisy drawing in of air. The inhalation remains calm, and its excessive depth is reduced mainly through relaxation.

If dryness appears during a session, you should check:

  • whether the inhalation has become too sharp;
  • whether air is being drawn in with effort;
  • whether the room is too dry;
  • whether there is congestion or irritation of the mucous membrane;
  • whether the chosen load is excessive.

There is no need to intensify the air shortage or to continue the exercise through pronounced discomfort.

What can be changed in everyday life

It is helpful to air the rooms, avoid overheating the air and, where possible, maintain comfortable humidity. Contact with tobacco smoke, construction dust and other irritants should be reduced.

Saline products are sometimes used to moisturize and care for the nasal cavity, but solutions differ in concentration and method of use. Frequent or incorrect rinsing can also irritate the mucous membrane. If you have a nasal disease, it is better to agree on the approach with a doctor.

Vasoconstrictor drops are not intended for uncontrolled long-term use.

When you need to see a doctor

A medical consultation is necessary if the dryness persists for a long time or is accompanied by frequent bleeding, pain, an unpleasant smell, pronounced crusting or persistent congestion.

You should also see a specialist if the problem arose after surgery, an injury or starting to take a medicine.

The main thing

During practice, the inhalation should not be sharp or noisy. Persistent or pronounced symptoms require finding out the cause, rather than attempts to overcome them with an increased breathing load.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous.
