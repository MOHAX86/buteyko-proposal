Body position and breathing

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. What helps you breathe more freely is not an “ideal” posture, but a stable position without excess tension and regular changes of posture.

Body position affects the movements of the chest, abdomen and breathing muscles. However, the link between posture and breathing cannot be reduced to a simple rule: “the straighter the back, the more correct the breathing.”

How posture changes breathing movements

During breathing, the volume of the chest cavity changes thanks to the work of the diaphragm and other breathing muscles. The position of the spine, ribs, shoulder girdle and abdomen affects how freely these movements are performed.

In a strongly bent position, the chest and abdomen may move less freely. If a person also stays motionless for a long time, tension in the neck and shoulders, fatigue and an urge to sigh deeply from time to time appear.

But an excessively straightened posture is not always helpful either. Trying to constantly draw the shoulders back, push out the chest and pull in the stomach creates unnecessary tension. Breathing may become noticeable and mainly upper-chest.

Breathing while sitting

For exercises and calm work, a stable position is suitable in which a person does not have to hold the body up with considerable effort. The feet can rest on the floor and the arms can lie freely.

It is not necessary to sit completely motionless. A slight change of position is natural. What matters is not folding the whole body forward and, at the same time, not turning a straight back into a tense stance.

During Buteyko practice, the position should help relaxation. If a posture causes pain or strong tension, it must be changed.

Breathing while standing and during movement

In a standing position, the demands on the postural muscles increase. If a person strongly tenses the abdomen, lower back or shoulder girdle, this is reflected in breathing.

During walking, posture changes constantly, and breathing adapts to the movement. A slight increase in ventilation is normal. There is no need to try to “fix” the torso in place or completely eliminate the movements of the chest.

Breathing while lying down

In a lying position, the relative arrangement of the organs and the effect of gravity change. On your back, the movements of the front abdominal wall usually become more noticeable. On your side, breathing movements may feel asymmetrical.

These differences in themselves do not indicate a disorder. A posture for rest and sleep should above all provide comfort and free breathing.

If marked breathlessness, attacks of suffocation or a need to sleep almost sitting up regularly occur in a horizontal position, you need to see a doctor.

Why it is important to change position

What benefits the body is not one “ideal” posture, but regular changes among the available positions. Prolonged immobility causes fatigue even with outwardly correct posture.

While working, you can periodically:

  • change your support;
  • stand up;
  • walk a little;
  • relax your shoulders and lower jaw;
  • check whether your mouth is open;
  • return to calm nasal breathing.

This is not a separate breathing exercise, but a way to remove the factors that maintain muscle tension.

The main thing

Correct posture is not a maximally straightened and motionless body, but a stable position without excess tension. Changing posture, relaxation and nasal breathing help to maintain a calmer breathing pattern during the day.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous.
