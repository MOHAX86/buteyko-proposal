How to observe your breathing during the day

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Several times a day, briefly notice whether your mouth is closed, whether your shoulders and abdomen are relaxed and whether there are frequent sighs. There is no need to control every breath.

Most of the time, a person does not notice their breathing. It is regulated automatically and changes along with movement, emotions, conversation, eating and mental work. That is why, to assess the breathing pattern, it is useful to observe not only how exercises are performed but also breathing in ordinary life.

What exactly you can notice

Several times a day you can calmly check:

  • whether you are breathing through the nose;
  • whether the mouth is open unnecessarily;
  • how noticeably the chest and abdomen move;
  • whether the shoulders rise on inhalation;
  • whether there is tension in the face, neck or abdomen;
  • whether frequent sighs have appeared;
  • whether a habit has arisen of taking a deep breath before a simple action;
  • whether breathing settles quickly after walking or talking.

These signs must not be used for self-diagnosis. They only help to see recurring habits and the situations in which breathing becomes more intense.

In which situations breathing changes most often

Breathing may intensify:

  • when in a hurry;
  • during an emotional conversation;
  • during intense work at the computer;
  • while watching alarming news;
  • after a heavy meal;
  • when climbing stairs;
  • in a stuffy or hot room;
  • with pain, fear or agitation;
  • while in an uncomfortable posture.

Not every increase is pathological. It is important to consider the cause and whether the response fits the situation. For example, when climbing stairs, ventilation should increase, and after you stop it should gradually decrease.

Observation and control are not the same thing

Observation means briefly noticing what is happening and, if necessary, removing excess tension. Rigid control looks different: a person tries to set the exact depth of every inhalation, constantly checks their sensations and is afraid of “breathing wrongly.”

Such an approach may increase anxiety and disrupt natural regulation. In the Buteyko method, work with breathing is built around relaxation and a gradual reduction of only the excess ventilation, not around continuous willful suppression of the inhalation.

Simple observation points

To avoid thinking about breathing all the time, you can link a brief check to several ordinary events:

  • after switching on the computer;
  • before a phone conversation;
  • after climbing stairs;
  • while stopped at a traffic light;
  • after eating;
  • before sleep.

It is enough to pay attention to the position of your mouth, relax your shoulders and check whether your breathing remains calm. There is no need to deliberately deepen the inhalation in order to check.

Do you need to write everything down

A detailed diary is useful during training, especially if an instructor evaluates the results. It may record the control pause, pulse, well-being, symptoms and the circumstances of the measurement.

For everyday observation, it is not necessary to record every change. It is more useful to note a stable pattern: for example, “I open my mouth while working” or “my breathing takes a long time to settle after an emotional conversation.”

The control pause also does not need to be measured many times in a row. Frequent checks can turn observation into chasing a result and distort the measurement.

The main thing

Observation helps to carry the skills of the Buteyko method into ordinary life. Its aim is not to control every breath, but to gradually make calm nasal breathing a natural habit.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous.
