How to switch from mouth breathing to nasal breathing

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. First find out whether the nose is clear and why the mouth opens. Then the skill is mastered gradually, through relaxation, without forcing the mouth to stay closed.

A habit of breathing through the mouth may persist even when the nasal passages are clear. But sometimes an open mouth is not a habit but a consequence of allergy, inflammation or an anatomical obstruction. So a safe transition to nasal breathing begins not with a willful ban, but with finding out the cause.

First you need to check whether the nose breathes freely

If one or both nostrils constantly do not let air through, or a person snores, wakes up with a dry mouth or feels air shortage with the mouth closed, it is necessary to see a doctor.

Nasal breathing may be hindered by:

  • infectious or allergic rhinitis;
  • marked swelling of the mucous membrane;
  • a deviated nasal septum;
  • polyps and other anatomical obstructions;
  • air that is too dry;
  • smoke, dust and chemical irritants;
  • excessive use of certain decongestants.

Breathing exercises cannot mechanically remove a polyp or straighten a septum. The Buteyko method does not replace examination and treatment of a diagnosed disease.

Observing breathing at rest

If the nose is passable, it is worth observing at which moments the mouth opens. This may happen during concentrated work, while looking at a phone, during conversation, walking or emotional tension.

There is no need to start controlling every breath right away. It is enough to calmly check several times a day:

  • whether the lips are closed without effort;
  • whether the lower jaw is loose;
  • whether the teeth are clenched;
  • whether the face, neck and shoulders are relaxed;
  • whether breathing remains quiet and unnoticeable.

The aim is not to force the mouth to stay closed, but to create conditions in which nasal breathing becomes natural.

Relaxation instead of force

An attempt to rigidly control breathing often increases muscle tension. A person presses their lips together, pulls in their stomach and starts suppressing every inhalation. Such a state cannot be considered normalization of breathing.

In the Buteyko method, excessive depth of breathing is reduced mainly through relaxation. The inhalation becomes less noticeable not because it is abruptly cut off, but because unnecessary tension decreases and the habit of inhaling more air than the body needs at that moment disappears.

A sensation of a slight, relatively comfortable air shortage is acceptable. Severe suffocation, panic and marked willful endurance indicate an excessive load.

The transition should be gradual

It is more convenient to start in calm situations: while sitting, reading or doing simple work. Then the skill is carried over to slow walking and ordinary household activities.

If marked discomfort arises quickly when the mouth is closed, you need to check:

  1. whether the nose is really clear;
  2. whether the person has become used to breathing too deeply and too often;
  3. whether they are trying to reduce breathing strongly at the same time;
  4. whether there is a disease that requires medical help.

You should not immediately demand nasal breathing during heavy exertion or try to maintain it at any cost.

Is it acceptable to tape the mouth at night

Taping the mouth cannot be regarded as the first way to restore nasal breathing. It is especially dangerous with a blocked nose, nausea, use of alcohol or sedatives, and also with possible sleep apnea.

First it is necessary to ensure free nasal breathing and find out why the mouth opens. Snoring, pauses in breathing, waking up choking and marked daytime sleepiness require examination.

The main thing

A safe transition to nasal breathing includes checking that the nose is passable, relaxing the breathing muscles, observing habits and gradually reducing only the excessive depth of breathing. The task is not to close the mouth by force, but to restore a calm, physiological breathing pattern.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
