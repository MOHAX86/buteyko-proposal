Sleeping with your mouth closed: what is important to know

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Sleeping with the mouth closed is possible only when the nasal and upper airways are clear. Taping the mouth without finding out the cause is unsafe, and snoring, pauses in breathing and pronounced daytime sleepiness require examination.

While awake, a person can keep an eye on the position of the lips and breathe through the nose. During sleep this control disappears, and if the nose is not clear enough or a habit has become entrenched, the mouth may open.

Dry mouth in the morning, snoring and feeling worn out sometimes point to mouth breathing at night, but the cause cannot be established from a single sign.

Why the mouth opens during sleep

Possible causes include:

  • nasal congestion;
  • an allergy or inflammation of the mucous membrane;
  • a deviated septum and other anatomical obstructions;
  • the position of the head and body;
  • relaxation of the lower jaw muscles;
  • a persistent habit of breathing through the mouth;
  • narrowing of the upper airways;
  • sleep-related breathing disorders.

During sleep, the regulation of breathing changes and the tone of a number of muscles decreases. A problem that is barely noticeable during the day may therefore be stronger at night.

Dry mouth and snoring

When air passes through the mouth, the mucous membrane loses moisture faster. A person may wake up with a dry mouth, an unpleasant taste or a desire to drink.

Snoring is caused by vibration of the tissues of the upper airways. It does not always mean apnea, but regular loud snoring cannot be regarded as merely a harmless sound.

The following require particular attention:

  • pauses in breathing noticed by others;
  • waking up with a feeling of suffocation;
  • a sharp snort after a pause;
  • frequent awakenings at night;
  • morning headaches;
  • pronounced daytime sleepiness;
  • reduced attention and ability to work.

If such signs are present, you need to see a doctor and, if necessary, undergo a sleep study.

Mouth breathing and deep breathing during sleep

From the standpoint of the Buteyko method, habitual features of the breathing pattern may intensify during sleep. It is easier to move large volumes of air through an open mouth, so mouth breathing can be accompanied by more pronounced ventilation.

However, it cannot be claimed that every episode of the mouth opening necessarily causes hyperventilation, or that all sleep disturbances are explained by a loss of carbon dioxide. Sleep apnea has complex mechanisms and requires medical diagnosis.

Should you tape your mouth

Taping the mouth does not remove the cause of a blocked nose, snoring or apnea. If the nose is not clear enough, restricting mouth breathing can be dangerous.

Taping should not be used:

  • when nasal breathing is difficult;
  • when sleep apnea is possible;
  • after drinking alcohol;
  • when taking substances that noticeably affect the level of consciousness;
  • in case of nausea or a risk of vomiting;
  • during acute diseases of the airways;
  • without prior assessment by a specialist.

The first step is to establish whether the person breathes freely through the nose and why the mouth opens during sleep.

What can be done safely

It is helpful to observe your nasal breathing during the day, maintain a comfortable indoor climate in the bedroom and seek medical help if the nose is constantly blocked.

Calm nasal breathing during the day can help form a stable habit, but it does not replace an examination for sleep disorders.

The main thing

Sleeping with the mouth closed is possible only when the nasal passages are clear and the upper airways are normally open. Snoring, pauses in breathing and pronounced daytime sleepiness require diagnosis. Forcibly closing or taping the mouth without finding out the cause is unsafe.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous.
