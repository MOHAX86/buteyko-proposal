Breathing while working at a computer

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. At the computer, breathing can change without being noticed: breath holds, frequent sighs and mouth breathing are possible. Brief check-ins, breaks and relaxation help.

Working at a computer seems to be a state of physical rest, yet breathing can change noticeably during it. Concentration, an emotional response to information, haste and staying motionless for a long time are often accompanied by muscle tension and disruption of the usual breathing rhythm.

That is why working time is an important part of the daily practice of normalizing breathing.

Why breathing changes at the computer

During intense work, attention switches entirely to the screen. A person stops noticing their body position and breathing. The head moves forward, the back rounds, the shoulders rise, and the muscles of the face and abdomen tense.

When reading a difficult text, filling in documents or waiting for an important message, brief breath holds may appear. After them, the body compensates with a stronger inhalation. If this keeps repeating, breathing becomes uneven: a breath hold gives way to a deep breath or a sigh.

A single sigh is not a disorder in itself. What deserves attention is a stable, repeating pattern, especially if it is accompanied by tension, dizziness, a feeling of air shortage or a rapid heartbeat.

Body position and breathing

There is no single ideal posture that must be kept all through the working day. Even a correct position becomes tiring if a person stays motionless for a long time.

For breathing, it is important that your posture does not hinder the movement of the chest and abdomen. The feet can rest steadily on the floor, the back can have support, and the shoulders and arms can stay relatively relaxed. It is better to position the screen so that you do not have to keep tilting your head.

There is no need to force yourself upright, push out your chest or pull in your stomach. An excessive effort to “hold your posture” may only increase muscle tension.

Brief observation instead of constant control

Several times during the working day, it is useful to check:

  • whether the mouth is closed without effort;
  • whether you are still inhaling through the nose;
  • whether the teeth are clenched;
  • whether the forehead and lower jaw are relaxed;
  • whether the shoulders are raised;
  • whether the stomach is pulled in;
  • whether there are frequent deep breaths and sighs.

After checking, there is no need to impose a strict rhythm on your breathing. It is enough to remove unnecessary tension and let the inhalation become calmer.

In the Buteyko method, reducing excessive depth of breathing is associated primarily with relaxation. If a person tries to rigidly restrict every inhalation, the work becomes yet another source of tension.

Why breaks are needed

Short breaks help to change body position and restore the natural breathing rhythm. You can stand up, walk around the room, look into the distance and relax your shoulders.

This does not have to be a separate exercise session: the task is to interrupt immobility and bring attention back to the body. After movement, breathing should gradually settle down.

If work causes strong emotional tension, it is useful to step away from the screen for a while. Several deliberately deep breaths do not always calm you: when ventilation is already increased, they can keep unpleasant sensations going. It is better to let breathing become quieter on its own while you relax.

The main thing

Brief check-ins, changes of position and relaxation help to maintain calm nasal breathing without constantly controlling every inhalation.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous.
