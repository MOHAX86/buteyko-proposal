Breathing during conversation: what to pay attention to

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Notice whether a habit has taken hold of taking excessively large inhalations, hurriedly gulping air or breathing deeply after a conversation. There is no need to suppress breathing.

Conversation inevitably changes the breathing rhythm. A person pronounces words mainly on the exhalation and inhales between phrases. The faster, louder and more emotional the speech, the more noticeable breathing may become.

This process is physiological in itself. What requires attention is not just any change in breathing, but an ingrained habit of taking excessively large inhalations, hurriedly gulping air or continuing to breathe deeply after the conversation has ended.

How speech changes breathing

In quiet silence, the length of inhalation and exhalation is regulated automatically. During speech, the exhalation lengthens and is broken up into sounds and words. The inhalation has to be taken in the pauses between phrases.

If a person speaks quickly and in long sentences, there is less time left to inhale. They may begin to pull air in sharply through the mouth, raise their shoulders or take too large a breath in advance.

Breathing is also affected by:

  • voice volume;
  • emotional state;
  • speed of speech;
  • sentence length;
  • talking while walking;
  • tension in the face, neck and abdomen.

The habit of “taking in as much air as possible”

An ordinary phrase does not always need the deepest possible inhalation. Yet some people seem to fill their lungs to the limit before starting to speak. If this happens all the time, ventilation may increase more than conversation requires.

In the Buteyko method, attention is paid to excessive depth of inhalation and unnecessary muscular effort. The task is not to pronounce a phrase while holding your breath, but to remove the unnecessary “reserve of air” through relaxation.

The inhalation before a phrase should remain natural. You should not deliberately restrict it to the point of marked discomfort.

What you can observe

During a conversation, it is useful to notice from time to time:

  • whether you inhale through the nose or through a wide-open mouth;
  • whether the shoulders rise;
  • whether the abdomen tightens;
  • whether you have to gulp air after every short phrase;
  • whether you manage to return to calm nasal breathing after the conversation;
  • whether breathing intensifies during agitation or an argument.

Such observation should be brief. If you constantly control every word and breath, your speech will become tense and unnatural.

Talking while walking

When walking and talking at the same time, ventilation increases for two reasons at once: the muscles are doing work, and the exhalation is used for speech. So the desire to inhale more often does not necessarily mean a disorder.

If a person finds it hard to finish a short phrase without marked breathlessness, they should slow down their walking pace. During intense exertion, conversation can no longer be treated as an ordinary everyday situation: breathing is subordinated to the needs of the working muscles.

How to restore breathing after a conversation

After emotional or prolonged speech, you can take a short pause, relax your face and shoulders and let your breathing settle on its own. There is no need to take several deep “recovery” breaths: they can keep ventilation increased.

If marked breathlessness, pain, dizziness or air shortage regularly occurs after an ordinary conversation, the cause cannot be explained by a breathing habit alone. A medical examination is necessary.

The main thing

Speech naturally changes inhalation and exhalation. The important thing is not to suppress breathing, but to notice unnecessary deep breaths and tension. After a conversation, breathing should gradually return to a calm nasal rhythm.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous.
