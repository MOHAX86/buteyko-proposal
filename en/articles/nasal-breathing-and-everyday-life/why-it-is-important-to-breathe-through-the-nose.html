Why it is important to breathe through the nose

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. The nose cleans, warms and humidifies the air and regulates its flow. In the Buteyko method, nasal breathing helps to observe breathing, but a closed mouth in itself does not guarantee normal breathing.

Breathing through the nose is the natural way for air to enter the airways. The nose does not simply let air through, but prepares it and takes part in protecting the lower airways.

In everyday life, nasal breathing also helps to maintain a calmer breathing pattern. That is why in the Buteyko method it is viewed as an important component of the normalization of breathing.

The nose is part of the respiratory system

Air passing through the nasal cavity comes into contact with the mucous membrane. Some of the dust and other airborne particles settle on it. Mucus traps them, and the cilia of the epithelium help to remove them afterward.

Thanks to the rich blood supply of the mucous membrane, inhaled air is warmed. At the same time, it is humidified. This preparation is especially important with cold or dry air: the bronchi and lungs receive air that better matches the conditions of the inner airways.

When inhaling through the mouth, a considerable part of this preparation is skipped. Air reaches the throat and lower airways faster, remaining colder and drier.

Why the nose affects airflow

The nasal passages create a natural resistance to the movement of air. This is not a flaw, but a normal feature of the respiratory system. The flow becomes more manageable, and the air stays in contact with the mucous membrane longer.

Air passes more easily through an open mouth. That is why mouth breathing makes it possible to increase ventilation volume quickly. This may be necessary during intense physical exercise, but at rest such a habit is often accompanied by more noticeable and larger-volume breathing.

A single inhalation through the mouth does not in itself mean hyperventilation. It is important to assess the whole breathing pattern: the depth and rate of breathing, the person’s condition and whether ventilation matches the body’s current need.

Nasal breathing and the Buteyko method

In the Buteyko method, the work is aimed not at reducing any breathing whatsoever, but at normalizing ventilation if it has become excessive. Calm breathing through the nose helps a person to observe the breathing pattern and avoid habitual big inhalations through the mouth.

However, a closed mouth in itself does not guarantee normal breathing. A person can breathe deeply and frequently through the nose too. That is why nasal breathing is considered together with relaxation, reducing only the excessive depth of inhalation and observing general condition.

When mouth breathing needs attention

The mouth may open temporarily during intense exertion, extreme heat or a badly blocked nose. This does not always indicate a disorder.

What deserves attention is persistent breathing through the mouth at rest, during ordinary walking or during sleep. The cause may be an allergy, inflammation of the mucous membrane, an anatomical obstruction or an entrenched habit. If the nose is constantly blocked, an examination by an ENT doctor is necessary.

A person must not be forced to keep the mouth closed if the nasal passages are not clear enough. First, the cause of the impaired nasal breathing needs to be established.

The main thing

In the Buteyko method, nasal breathing is one of the conditions for forming a calm breathing pattern, but it is applied without force and only when the nose is clear.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
