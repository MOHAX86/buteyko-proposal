How to make calm breathing an everyday habit

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Choose two or three recurring situations, briefly check your mouth, shoulders and inhalation in them, and return to relaxation. Round-the-clock control is not needed.

Breathing practice takes a limited amount of time, but a person breathes around the clock. So a lasting result depends not only on doing the exercises, but also on what the breathing pattern remains like during work, walking, conversations, rest and sleep.

The task is not constant control, but the gradual formation of calm nasal breathing as a natural habit.

What calm breathing means

At rest, breathing usually goes through the nose and requires no noticeable muscular effort. The face, shoulders and abdomen remain relatively relaxed, and inhalation is not accompanied by a constant rise of the upper chest.

This does not mean that breathing should always have the same depth and rate. It naturally changes with movement, conversation, emotions and physical exertion.

In the Buteyko method, normalization of breathing means reducing ventilation only when it is excessive relative to the needs of the body. The aim is not an arbitrary buildup of carbon dioxide or reducing breathing below the physiological norm.

Start with a few situations

There is no need to try to change the whole day at once. It is more convenient to choose two or three recurring situations:

  • working at the computer;
  • calm walking;
  • looking at your phone;
  • waiting for public transport;
  • resting after a meal;
  • getting ready for sleep.

At these moments you can briefly check whether your mouth is closed, whether your shoulders are relaxed and whether an unnecessary deep breath has appeared.

When the new skill becomes habitual in one situation, it is carried over to the next.

Remove the causes that intensify breathing

The everyday breathing pattern depends not only on a person’s wishes. It can be changed by:

  • haste;
  • stress;
  • an uncomfortable posture;
  • pain;
  • a high temperature;
  • overeating;
  • lack of sleep;
  • exertion that is too intense;
  • nasal congestion;
  • diseases of the respiratory and cardiovascular systems.

If these factors are not taken into account, the attempt to “breathe correctly” turns into a constant struggle. Sometimes it is more important to change body position, slow your walking pace or see a doctor than to intensify a breathing exercise.

Return to relaxation

When you notice deep or mouth breathing, there is no need to cut off the inhalation abruptly. First relax your face, lower jaw, neck, shoulders and abdomen.

After that, breathing is allowed to become less noticeable. A slight, relatively comfortable air shortage is acceptable, but marked suffocation and prolonged willful endurance are not the aim of the practice.

If a person constantly controls every inhalation, gets anxious over any sigh and is afraid to open their mouth even during heavy exertion, the approach needs to be reconsidered.

Breathing during movement and conversation

During walking, ventilation should increase. Nasal inhalation can be used as a guide to a manageable intensity, but it should not be maintained at any cost.

During conversation, inhalations are naturally distributed between phrases. There is no need to speak while holding your breath. It is enough to avoid the habit of taking in an excessively large volume of air each time, and to return to calm nasal breathing after you finish speaking.

How to assess changes

The control pause helps to observe changes over time, but it should not turn into a competition. The measurement is taken under comparable conditions, until the first desire to inhale, without maximum endurance.

Progress may also be indicated by:

  • calmer breathing at rest;
  • fewer habitual deep sighs;
  • keeping inhalation through the nose during ordinary walking;
  • quicker recovery after exertion;
  • better tolerance of everyday activities;
  • positive changes in well-being.

A single drop in the control pause does not mean that all progress has been lost. The value is affected by sleep, stress, illness, exertion, time of day and how correctly it is measured.

The role of training

General articles help to understand the direction of the work, but they do not make it possible to select an individual load. With chronic diseases, marked symptoms or low tolerance of air shortage, it is safer to learn the practice under the guidance of a trained instructor.

Medicines must not be stopped or changed on your own. Breathing practice does not replace medical diagnosis and prescribed treatment.

The main thing

A habit is formed by repetition in ordinary situations. The guideposts are free nasal inhalation at rest, relaxation and the absence of unnecessary deep breaths. The practice should not turn into round-the-clock suppression of breathing.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
