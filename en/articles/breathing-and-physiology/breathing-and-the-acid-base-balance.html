Breathing and the acid-base balance

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Breathing quickly affects the level of CO2 and blood pH: with excessive ventilation, pH rises, and with insufficient ventilation, it falls.

What acid-base balance means

Enzymes, membranes, and metabolic reactions work normally only within a fairly narrow range of acidity. That is why the body regulates blood pH with the help of buffer systems, the lungs, and the kidneys. These mechanisms act at different speeds and complement one another.

The most important one for understanding breathing is the bicarbonate system. In simplified form, it can be pictured as a reversible chain: CO2 combines with water, forms carbonic acid, and then hydrogen ions and bicarbonate. The reactions are accelerated by the enzyme carbonic anhydrase.

How breathing changes pH

The lungs regulate the amount of volatile acid by removing CO2. With excessive ventilation, CO2 falls, the equilibrium shifts toward fewer hydrogen ions, and pH rises. This is respiratory alkalosis.

With insufficient ventilation, CO2 accumulates, there are more hydrogen ions, and pH falls. This primary shift is called respiratory acidosis. In clinical practice, the diagnosis is not made by judging the breathing by eye but from the overall picture of the condition and laboratory values.

The fast role of the lungs and the slow role of the kidneys

A change in ventilation can affect CO2 within minutes. The kidneys respond more slowly. They change the excretion of hydrogen ions and the handling of bicarbonate, helping to bring pH closer to the normal range.

With a chronic decrease in CO2, the kidneys excrete more bicarbonate; with a chronic increase in CO2, they retain it and increase acid excretion. This is called compensation. It does not necessarily normalize pH completely and does not eliminate the primary cause of the breathing disorder.

Why acute and chronic states differ

At the same PaCO2, pH may differ depending on how long the disorder has lasted and the degree of renal compensation. That is why a single number cannot be interpreted out of context. A doctor assesses pH, PaCO2, bicarbonate, symptoms, the history of the condition, and other values.

Urine acidity is also not a direct copy of blood pH. It is affected by diet, kidney function, medicines, hydration, and the time of collection. It is wrong to draw a conclusion about systemic acidosis or alkalosis from a urine test strip alone.

The connection with the Buteyko method

In the school’s methodological position, excessive ventilation is regarded as a factor that lowers CO2. The work is aimed at normalizing breathing to the physiological norm. This does not mean treating all pH disorders on your own with breathing exercises.

The control pause is used in practice as a functional indicator of changes over time, but it does not replace a blood gas analysis and does not measure pH. If a person has a severe disease of the lungs, kidneys, heart, or metabolism, interpreting the acid-base state is a medical task.

Brief conclusion

Breathing affects pH quickly through CO2, while the kidneys take part in compensation more slowly. Understanding this connection helps to explain the physiology, but it does not make it possible to make a diagnosis from breathing sensations or the control pause.
