Control pause and how you feel: how to keep observations

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Record the control pause together with well-being, familiar symptoms, energy and the circumstances of the day. What matters are combinations that recur across several comparable observations on different days, not a chance coincidence of one figure and one sensation.

Why a single CP value is not enough

A number does not convey how a person slept, what they felt and what events preceded the measurement.

Well-being as an independent part of observation

An improvement in general condition is important in itself and should not get lost behind attention to the stopwatch.

What to note along with the control pause

It is enough to briefly record general condition, energy, familiar symptoms and unusual circumstances.

General condition and energy

For example: “usual vigor”, “weakness stronger than usual”, “calmer after practice”.

Familiar symptoms

Note the presence and severity of already known manifestations without determining their cause yourself.

Sleep, stress, exertion and other circumstances

Write down only what noticeably sets the day apart: lack of sleep, severe stress, unusual exertion or the onset of feeling unwell.

How to word observations

Entries should contain facts and be repeated in the same form.

Facts instead of general judgments

“Headache 3 out of 10 in the morning” is more useful than “I feel bad”.

The same scales and short entries

For a recurring symptom, use one scale, for example from 0 to 10.

How to compare the CP and well-being

Look through several days and note whether the CP, symptoms and general condition are moving in agreement.

Look for a trend over several days

Example: “MCP around its previous level for three days, sleep has improved, the usual cough is weaker”.

Do not explain every change by a single cause

An example of a correct entry: “MCP lower than usual; slept 5 hours; I am not determining the cause yet”.

When an observation requires attention

A persistent simultaneous worsening of the indicator and of your condition is worth discussing. If the decline recurs and familiar symptoms intensify, show your records to the instructor.

Pronounced or new symptoms

New, pronounced or alarming symptoms require medical assessment, not just observation of breathing.

How to discuss your records with the instructor

Pass on the dates, MCP values and a short description of symptoms, practice and circumstances. Do not hide any worsening and do not try to increase the load on your own.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
