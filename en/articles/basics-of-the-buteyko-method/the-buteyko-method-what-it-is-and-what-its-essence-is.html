The Buteyko method: what it is and what its essence is

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. The Buteyko method (VEDB) is a system in which, during training sessions, people learn to reduce the depth of breathing through relaxation, down to a slight air shortage. The goal is to bring breathing to normal.

The Buteyko method is an advanced medical technology for normalizing the gas constants of the respiratory gases in the body: the content of oxygen and carbon dioxide in the lungs, blood and cells. Normalization is achieved through step-by-step work on reducing breathing to the physiological norm, mainly through relaxation, as well as through work with factors that deepen breathing.

A brief definition of the Buteyko method

The method is called VEDB, Voluntary Elimination of Deep Breathing. It does not come down to a single breath hold or a single exercise. It is a system of training in which a person understands the goal, masters practical actions, receives assignments and observes changes over time.

The reference point is not the smallest breathing but normal breathing. Correct practice is therefore not built on records and hard endurance.

What the method calls deep breathing

Deep breathing is understood as ventilation of the lungs that is excessive relative to the body’s current metabolic needs. It is not necessarily one very large or noisy inhalation. A person may appear to breathe calmly yet maintain an excessive minute volume.

The need for ventilation changes along with one’s condition and exertion. Increased breathing during movement cannot automatically be considered a disorder; what is assessed is whether breathing matches the situation.

What the basic principle of the method is

The work is aimed at gradually reducing excessive ventilation to the physiological norm. The main means are observation, relaxation, manageable practice and removing the influence of factors that deepen breathing.

The “left-hand rule” used by K. P. Buteyko helps you remember the principle: reducing / the depth / of breathing / by relaxing the diaphragm / to the point of air shortage. It does not replace training and does not describe all the details of the method.

Observing your breathing

A person learns to notice the depth and rate of breathing, unnecessary muscular effort, and the response to emotions, physical load and familiar circumstances.

Relaxation

The depth of inhalation is reduced primarily through relaxation. Trying to suppress breathing by force increases tension and can lead to incorrect performance. When a slight air shortage appears, relax within it, without struggling.

Gradual reduction of excess ventilation

The load is chosen so that breathing remains manageable. The excess is removed in stages, not by one sharp effort.

What elements the practice consists of

The practice includes theoretical preparation, assessment of the initial condition, learning to relax and reduce breathing, a schedule of sessions, homework and subsequent correction. Factors that deepen breathing are considered separately: situations and influences under which breathing becomes deeper again. You learn to reduce breathing during training, while in everyday activities you monitor your condition and relax without deliberately reducing your breathing.

What the control pause is used for

The control pause is the duration of a breath hold at the end of an ordinary, unaccentuated exhalation. It is measured after several minutes of rest and ends at the first desire to inhale. The hold should not turn into strenuous endurance, and after it breathing should remain practically the same as before the measurement.

The CP helps you observe changes over time, but it is not a competition. The school assesses it together with changes in symptoms and general well-being.

How training usually begins

First there is a conversation and a medical history is taken, and possible causes of the breathing disorder and factors that deepen breathing are identified. Then the person receives theoretical preparation. According to the protocol, a deep-breathing test may be carried out, after which training in relaxation and in reducing breathing begins. Next, a schedule and homework are given, and at subsequent meetings the instructor checks and corrects the performance.

How the method differs from ordinary breathing exercises

Breathing exercises are a broad concept that brings together different approaches. What distinguishes the Buteyko method is its focus on normalizing excess ventilation, the leading role of relaxation, and work not only during the exercise but also with the everyday breathing habit.

Can you start on your own

General information helps you understand the goal and the terms of the method. However, the school strongly advises against learning the practice on your own, without an instructor: there are many subtleties, and actions that look similar from outside can produce different results.

Briefly about safety and the limits of the method

The practice should not be accompanied by severe suffocation, marked worsening or intensified symptoms. If such signs appear, the exercise is stopped. An informational article does not replace individual assessment, diagnosis and medical care, and is not grounds for changing treatment on your own.

Key points about the Buteyko method

The essence of the method lies neither in breath holds nor in breathing as little as possible, but in the step-by-step normalization of breathing and gas parameters to the physiological norm, mainly through relaxation and work with the causes of deeper breathing.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
