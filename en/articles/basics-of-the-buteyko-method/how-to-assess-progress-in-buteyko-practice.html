How to assess progress in Buteyko practice

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Progress is not a breath-holding record. The school assesses it by a sustained trend in the control pause, easing of symptoms, improvement in general condition and correct performance of the practice.

The maximum number may be obtained through endurance and not reflect the quality of practice.

Why a single number can be misleading

The CP is affected by time, one’s condition, and circumstances and technique.

A sustained trend instead of a maximum

Assess a comparable series of MCP values over weeks.

Control pause trend

Look for a sustained direction without requiring growth every day.

Symptom trend

Familiar symptoms should ease, diminish or disappear.

General well-being and energy

A positive sign is an improvement in condition, the appearance of energy and a calmer state of well-being.

How to assess the quality of a single exercise

Compare the CP, symptoms and condition before and after.

CP before and after practice

In the school’s methodological materials, a correctly performed exercise is expected to be followed by an increase in the CP of about 5 seconds or more, often 5–10 seconds. This is a benchmark, not a guarantee.

Changes in symptoms and condition

The exercise should be accompanied by easing of symptoms and improvement in condition.

When worsening points to a mistake

Pronounced discomfort, intensification of symptoms, large recovery breaths or worsening require stopping and checking the technique.

How to assess long-term trends

The basis is the MCP and a weekly diary review.

Compare identical conditions and take into account symptoms, condition and circumstances.

A benchmark of +1–2 seconds per week

With regular practice, the school uses an approximate benchmark of MCP growth of 1–2 seconds per week. This is not a mandatory schedule or a promise of results.

Plateaus, setbacks and nonlinearity

Temporary standstills and fluctuations are possible. What matters is what is happening with the overall direction and well-being.

Which signs matter more than the figure

Calm breathing in daily life, tolerance of ordinary activity and a reduction in familiar symptoms show that the skill is carrying over into life.

Calmer breathing in daily life

The person notices deepening of breathing earlier and returns to relaxation more easily.

Tolerance of ordinary activity

Everyday exertion is tolerated more calmly, within the limits of the individual’s condition.

Reduction of familiar symptoms

Record recurring changes without turning them into a self-made diagnosis.

When to adjust the practice

Correction is needed when there is no positive trend, when symptoms intensify or when there is pronounced discomfort.

No positive trend

The instructor checks the technique, load, regularity and factors that deepen breathing.

Intensified symptoms or pronounced discomfort

Do not increase the effort and do not continue through hard endurance.

The role of the instructor

The instructor helps to distinguish a performance mistake from a temporary fluctuation and adjusts the assignments. If you have a disease, an improvement in the indicators does not cancel the need for medical supervision.

Summary scheme for assessing progress

Collect a comparable series of MCP values, compare symptoms, assess well-being and energy, check the response to the exercises and discuss the sustained trend with your instructor. Progress is confirmed by a combination of signs, not by a single figure.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
