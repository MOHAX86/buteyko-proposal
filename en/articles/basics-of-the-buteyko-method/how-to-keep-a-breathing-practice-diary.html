How to keep a breathing practice diary

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. A useful diary is brief, regular and uniform. It links the morning control pause (MCP), the pulse if necessary, the practice done, symptoms, well-being and important circumstances of the day.

Why a diary is needed

Records preserve the facts: without a diary, isolated vivid days are easily mistaken for a general trend. Consecutive entries make it easier to see where correction is needed and to discuss this with the instructor.

Minimum daily records

Date, time, MCP, a brief assessment of well-being and the practice done.

Date, time and MCP

The main series is the morning values under comparable conditions.

Pulse and well-being

The pulse is recorded if necessary, at the same state of rest; well-being is described briefly.

Practice and its duration

Write down the name of the exercise you have mastered and the actual time, without adding unnecessary details.

What to record additionally

Only significant symptoms and circumstances that set the day apart.

Symptoms before and after practice

Rate the severity of a familiar symptom in the same way each time.

Sleep, stress, exertion and unusual circumstances

Note lack of sleep, severe stress, unusual exertion or feeling unwell.

How to word entries

Write briefly, specifically and in the same words. For example: “slept 5 hours”, not “the night was terrible”.

One scale for recurring symptoms

Use one scale, for example 0–10, without changing it.

Sample entry for one day

Short version

September 4, 07:30. MCP 24 s. Well-being as usual. Practice 20 minutes.

Extended version

September 4, 07:30. MCP 24 s, pulse 68. Sleep 6 hours. Nasal congestion 3/10 before practice, 1/10 after. Practice 20 minutes. Stressful day.

How to review the diary

Once a week, assess the overall series without chasing a record and without picking only the best result.

See whether the direction of changes in MCP, symptoms and condition repeats.

Which entries to show the instructor

Show the period with unusual changes, the technique, the load and the circumstances.

Typical diary-keeping mistakes

Too many fields, irregularity, recording only records, mixing morning and daytime pauses and attempting to diagnose yourself. The diary is needed for observation and correction.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
