Frequently asked questions about the Buteyko method

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. In the Buteyko method, excess breathing is reduced to the norm mainly through relaxation. The method is not limited to breath holds, and the school strongly advises against practicing without an instructor.

Below are brief answers for a first acquaintance. They do not replace individual training.

What is the Buteyko method?

It is an advanced medical technology for normalizing the gas constants of the respiratory gases in the body. Normalization is achieved by a step-by-step reduction of excess breathing to the physiological norm, mainly through relaxation and work with factors that deepen breathing.

What does VEDB mean?

VEDB stands for “Voluntary Elimination of Deep Breathing”. Will shows itself in the conscious organization of training and practice. What is eliminated is not breathing, but only its excess depth, down to the norm.

What counts as deep breathing?

In the school’s terminology, this is lung ventilation that is excessive relative to the body’s current metabolic needs. It does not necessarily look like a very big or noisy inhalation.

Do you need to hold your breath?

The method is not limited to breath holds. The control pause is used to observe the state of breathing and ends before strenuous willful endurance. The maximum pause is a different measurement.

What sensations are acceptable during practice?

A relatively comfortable air shortage is possible. Severe suffocation, marked tension, intensified symptoms and worsening of your condition are considered signs of a mistake and require stopping the exercise.

Where should a beginner start?

With understanding the goal, a conversation and an initial assessment. Then observation and relaxation are learned, and manageable tasks are given and performed regularly, with subsequent correction.

Can you practice on your own?

You can study the theory on your own. The school strongly advises against practicing without an instructor: your own mistakes are hard to notice without feedback.

How long does training take?

There is no single time frame. It depends on the initial condition, regularity, accuracy of performance, factors that deepen breathing and the quality of correction. A quick change in a single indicator does not yet equal a stable skill.

How does the method differ from breathing exercises?

“Breathing exercises” is a broad concept. What distinguishes the Buteyko method is the reduction of excess ventilation to the norm, the leading role of relaxation, comprehensive monitoring of changes over time and transferring the skill into everyday life.

How can you tell that a stable skill is forming?

The person is better at noticing when breathing deepens, is able to relax and dose the load, restores the breathing pattern more calmly and applies the learned principles outside the exercise. Progress is assessed by the CP, symptoms and general well-being, not by a single record.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
