The Buteyko method for beginners: where to start

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Begin by understanding the goal and calmly observing your breathing, and learn the practice with an instructor. The main thing at the start is relaxation, not a struggle with breathing.

It is important for a newcomer not to rush into technique. Without understanding the goal, an attempt to “breathe less” easily turns into tension.

First understand the goal of the method

The goal is to normalize breathing and gas values, not to reduce breathing as much as possible. Excessive ventilation is reduced only to the physiological norm and mainly through relaxation.

Observe your breathing without trying to change it right away

A few minutes of calm observation help you see your habitual pattern. You can notice the movement of the shoulders and chest, the involvement of the abdomen, unnecessary muscular effort, the audibility of breathing and its response to emotions. At this stage there is no need to forcibly correct every breath.

Learn correct self-observation

You should observe not only your breathing but also your well-being, symptoms and the circumstances that deepen breathing. To track changes over time, the school uses the control pause. It must be measured at rest and without hard endurance.

Start with relaxation, not with a struggle against breathing

Reducing the inhalation by means of tension is a common mistake. The right direction is to relax the respiratory muscles and remove unnecessary effort, so that the air shortage remains slight. If after an attempt you have to recover your breath greedily, the load was excessive or the technique was performed incorrectly.

Choose a manageable format of practice

The format should take the person’s condition into account. The school’s methodological base strongly advises against learning the practice without an instructor: outside observation, feedback and correction are important. An informational text can prepare you for training but cannot replace individual selection.

Keep simple records of your sessions

It is useful to record the morning control pause, completion of assignments, well-being and noticeable factors that deepen breathing. The records are needed not for a daily competition but for calmly observing the trend.

Avoid records and forcing

The quality of a session must not be judged by the strength of the suffocation or the length of the maximum breath hold. Pronounced discomfort, intensified symptoms and worsening of your condition are signs that you need to stop and check your performance.

On your own or with an instructor

On your own, you can study the definitions and prepare questions. The practical fine points are best learned under observation. The instructor sees tension, helps to dose the load, checks home practice and assesses not a single figure but the whole trend.

First steps: a short route

First, understand the goal of the method. Then have a conversation and an initial assessment, learn to observe your breathing, master relaxation and receive manageable exercises and a schedule. After that, practice regularly, keep records and come back for correction.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
