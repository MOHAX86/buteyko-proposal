Control pause and maximum pause: what is the difference

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. The control pause and the maximum pause belong to one breath hold but end at different stages. The CP ends at the first signs of a need to inhale, while the MP continues further and requires substantial voluntary effort.

Two parts of one breath hold

First comes the relatively calm control part, then the voluntary section begins.

The boundary of the control part

It is the first distinct signs of a need to resume breathing.

The start of the voluntary section

Beyond this boundary, continuing requires active endurance.

Control pause

The CP is moderate and should not noticeably disturb the breathing that follows.

Sensations and the criterion for ending

A relatively comfortable lack of air is acceptable, but not severe suffocation.

Breathing after the CP

It remains practically the same as before the hold.

Maximum pause

The MP includes a further section of the hold and pronounced effort.

The role of voluntary effort

The person continues the hold beyond the control boundary.

Why recovery after it is more pronounced

A greater load more often causes a noticeable recovery breath and faster breathing.

Purpose of the measurement

The CP is needed for comparable observation; the MP is a different methodological measurement.

Why the MP must not be recorded as the CP

Doing so artificially inflates the number, and the diary loses comparability.

Artificial inflation of the result

The extra seconds reflect endurance, not the control criterion.

Loss of diary comparability

You cannot compare a moderate CP from one day with a maximum breath hold from another.

Does a beginner need to measure the maximum pause

The MP is not a required measurement to take on your own. It should not be trained on the basis of a general article; whether it is needed and how it is done is determined during training.

It is more important for a newcomer to learn to recognize the CP accurately and to keep breathing calm.

Brief reminder

CP: first need to inhale, moderation, calm breathing. MP: continuation, substantial effort, more pronounced recovery.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
