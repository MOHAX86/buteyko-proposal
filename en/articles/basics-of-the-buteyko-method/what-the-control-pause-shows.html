What the control pause shows

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. In Buteyko practice, the CP serves as a guide to the state of breathing and its changes across repeated comparable measurements. A single number is not a diagnosis or a complete assessment of health.

The control pause as a functional indicator

The CP reflects a person’s response to stopping breathing after an ordinary exhalation and the moment when the need to inhale again appears.

Sensitivity to the need to resume breathing

The measurement depends on the current breathing pattern, sensations and correct determination of where the pause ends.

A practical reference point in the Buteyko method

The indicator helps you observe changes during training and discuss them with an instructor.

Why the trend of the control pause matters

Repeated measurements make it possible to compare a person primarily with themselves.

Comparing a person with themselves

Someone else’s number does not take into account differences in conditions, state and technique.

Identical measurement conditions

Without a similar time, posture and end criterion, a change in seconds may reflect the way of measuring rather than a stable trend.

What can affect the result

What matters is your state before the measurement, stress, fatigue, physical load, sleep, well-being and technique errors.

State before the measurement

Recent activity or emotional excitement make the result situational.

Stress, fatigue, physical load and well-being

These circumstances may be accompanied by a change in the CP, but a cause cannot be established from a single coincidence.

Technique errors

A deep inhalation, a forced exhalation or willful prolonging distort comparability.

What the control pause does not show by itself

The CP does not establish a disease, does not describe the whole of gas exchange and does not replace an examination.

Why it is not a diagnosis

The same number may occur with different conditions, while a diagnosis requires other data.

Why one number does not describe the whole of gas exchange

The composition of alveolar air or blood gases cannot be accurately determined from a single breath hold.

Why the result cannot be assessed out of context

The value needs to be compared with the conditions, well-being and repeated observations.

How to use the indicator correctly

The CP is considered together with pulse, familiar symptoms and general condition, without deriving a diagnosis from their combination.

The CP together with pulse, symptoms and well-being

These data complement one another and help you see the context.

Observing the trend

A series of correct measurements is useful, not a single maximum.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
