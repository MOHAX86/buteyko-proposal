The Buteyko control pause: what it is

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. The control pause is a calm breath hold after an ordinary exhalation until the first distinct signs of a need to inhale. It is needed for observing trends, not for records or diagnoses.

The control pause is one of the main indicators by which the state of breathing is observed in the Buteyko method. It ends before hard endurance begins.

A simple definition of the control pause

The control pause shows how long a person can calmly go without breathing after an ordinary exhalation, without taking the hold to the point of pronounced discomfort. The task is to recognize the same moderate end point every time, not to obtain the maximum number.

After which exhalation the count begins

Counting begins at the end of an ordinary, calm and unaccentuated exhalation. Before the hold, there is no need to inhale deeply, deliberately take in air or try to breathe out everything possible from the lungs. A forced exhalation changes the starting conditions.

By what sensation the pause ends

The pause ends at the first distinct signs of a need to resume breathing. This may be felt as a moderate lack of air or as the first involuntary breathing movements. Continuing would already require noticeable voluntary effort.

What a correctly completed control pause looks like

If the pause is ended in time, the person resumes breathing calmly. They do not need a long recovery or several deep breaths.

The acceptable feeling of air shortage

A relatively comfortable air shortage is acceptable. It should not turn into severe suffocation, agonizing endurance or a struggle with yourself.

How breathing should remain after the pause

The depth and rate of breathing remain practically the same as before the hold. A big, sharp recovery breath indicates that the pause was held too long.

Why the control pause is used in the Buteyko method

The control pause supplements sensations with a numerical indicator that can be recorded and compared. The method itself does not come down to breath holds: its goal is to normalize breathing to the physiological norm mainly through relaxation and work with factors that deepen breathing.

An indicator for observing the state of breathing

The CP is affected by practice, stress, fatigue, sleep, exertion, feeling unwell and the measurement conditions. That is why it is considered together with well-being and symptoms.

The importance of the trend rather than a single number

A sustained trend under comparable conditions is useful. A randomly high pause does not necessarily mean improvement, nor does a one-off drop mean worsening.

What the control pause is not

It is a mistake to turn the CP into a record, a medical test or the only goal of practice.

Not a competition or a test of willpower

Extra seconds gained through tension do not make the measurement more correct. Relaxation, accuracy and comparability are what matter.

Not a maximum breath hold

The control pause ends earlier than the maximum one: the person does not reach the hard voluntary section.

Not a diagnosis in its own right

A single CP cannot be used to make a diagnosis, to determine the gas content of the blood precisely or to assess health exhaustively.

What you need to know before the first measurement

It is better to treat the first measurement as getting acquainted with the sensation, not as a test of your abilities.

Rest, relaxation and identical conditions

A few minutes of rest are needed before measuring. For long-term observation, the school mainly uses the morning control pause (MCP) under similar conditions.

Why the detailed technique is covered in a separate article

The full procedure is covered separately. The school recommends learning the practice with an instructor, who helps you recognize the criterion for ending and unnoticed tension.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
