How training in the Buteyko method begins

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Training begins not with immediate breath holds but with understanding the initial condition, the possible causes of the disturbance and the logic of the method. Then the person masters relaxation, and only after that learns to reduce excess breathing.

Where acquaintance with the method begins

The first stage is a conversation and taking the medical history. The instructor finds out what troubles the person, when their well-being changes, how their day is structured and what influences may deepen breathing. At the same time, introductory theoretical preparation is provided.

In this way, practice does not turn into a set of actions without a clear goal. A person should know that breathing is reduced not to a minimum but to the physiological norm.

Observing habitual breathing

A newcomer learns to notice breathing in a calm state: how audible it is, which muscles take part, whether the shoulders rise, whether there is unnecessary tension. Then they observe how breathing changes under the influence of conversation, movement, emotions and other circumstances.

Baseline indicators and self-observation

The control pause, symptoms and general well-being are used for observation. The CP is measured after several minutes of rest, at the end of an ordinary, unaccentuated exhalation, without hard voluntary endurance. It is desirable to take measurements under the same conditions; for tracking changes over time, the MCP is mainly used.

Recording the indicators helps to see the process over time, but a figure does not replace an assessment of the person’s condition.

Mastering relaxation

Relaxation is the practical basis for reducing the depth of inhalation. A person learns to remove tension from the face, shoulders, abdomen and respiratory muscles. If the reduction is achieved by clamping down, it contradicts the principle of the method.

First acquaintance with reducing the depth of breathing

After mastering relaxation, the person tries to reduce unnecessary breathing effort. The load remains manageable. A slight, relatively comfortable air shortage is acceptable, but not severe, distressing sensations.

Under the training protocol, a deep-breathing test may be carried out: ventilation is increased, changes in well-being and symptoms are observed, and then, after breathing returns to normal, the reverse changes are assessed. Such a test belongs to training with an instructor and is not a universal instruction for doing it on your own.

How a manageable load is selected

The instructor takes into account the initial condition, the response to practice, and changes in the CP, symptoms and well-being. If a person has to recover their breathing abruptly, strains excessively or feels worse, the load is reconsidered.

Home practice and observation records

After training, a person receives a schedule and homework. In the records, they can note the MCP, completed sessions, well-being and important circumstances of the day. This helps to see the effect of regularity and of factors that deepen breathing.

Which mistakes are important to notice at the start

Typical mistakes include forcibly reducing breathing, chasing the pause time, an excessive feeling of suffocation, continuing an exercise when feeling worse and trying to increase the load on your own. Another mistake is doing the sessions but not changing the everyday factors that constantly deepen breathing.

What to do after the first stage

At subsequent meetings, the instructor checks performance, corrects the exercises and assesses the effect. Gradually, the person learns to carry out assignments more precisely and to carry the mastered principles into ordinary life. The initial stage ends not with a record but with an understanding of the method and the emergence of a stable, safe way of practicing.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
