How long it takes to master the Buteyko method

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. There is no single time frame. Mastery depends on the initial condition, regularity, accuracy of performance, factors that deepen breathing and timely correction.

Why there is no single time frame

People start with different breathing habits, symptoms and lifestyles. One quickly understands the principle but takes a long time to learn to relax. Another performs the exercise correctly, but between sessions faces severe stress or other factors that deepen breathing.

Therefore a promise of the same result by a particular day does not reflect the real learning process.

What it means to “master the method”

Mastery consists of several levels. Knowing the name of an exercise is not enough.

Understand the principle

The person understands that only excess breathing is reduced, and only to the physiological norm. They distinguish relaxation from forcible suppression, and the control pause from the maximum pause.

Learn to perform the practice

The next level is to relax correctly, dose the load, do the homework and assess the response by the control pause, symptoms and well-being.

Transfer the skill into everyday life

Stable mastery shows itself when a person notices factors that deepen breathing outside sessions and applies the principles learned in ordinary situations.

What the speed of learning depends on

It is influenced by the initial condition, regularity, understanding of the theory, the ability to relax, accuracy of performance and the quality of feedback. Sleep, movement, stress, emotions, nutrition and other circumstances from the open-ended list of factors that deepen breathing also matter.

How regularity affects mastering the skill

Rare intensive attempts do not replace consistent sessions. Regularity allows you to repeat the correct action, observe the trend and correct mistakes in time. In the methodological base, the benchmark for long-term growth of the CP with regular practice is about 1–2 seconds per week, but it is not a promise of a result for every person.

Why quick changes do not equal a stable skill

After a single correctly performed exercise, the CP may increase by about 5 seconds or more; a gain of 5–10 seconds is often considered. But a one-off change does not yet prove that the habit has changed. What matters is whether the trend persists and whether symptoms and general condition improve.

How to assess progress

The whole set of data is assessed: the control pause in comparable conditions, changes in symptoms, well-being, accuracy of performance and the ability to apply the skill in life. One bad day or one high value should not determine the whole conclusion.

When the way of practicing should be reconsidered

The practice is reconsidered if a person regularly experiences marked discomfort, takes big recovery breaths, notes intensified symptoms or keeps repeating the same mistake for a long time. It may be necessary to reduce the load, refine the technique or change the homework together with the instructor.

A realistic approach to time frames

It is more sensible to ask not “in how many days will I master the method” but “which stage am I mastering now, and am I doing the work correctly”. Understanding may come quickly, while a stable skill requires consistency. The time frame is judged by real progress, not by a promised calendar date.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
