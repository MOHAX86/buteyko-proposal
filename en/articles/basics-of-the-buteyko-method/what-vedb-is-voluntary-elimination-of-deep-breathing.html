What VEDB is: Voluntary Elimination of Deep Breathing

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. VEDB is the Voluntary Elimination of Deep Breathing: consciously organized work that removes only the excess depth of breathing, down to the norm and mainly through relaxation.

VEDB is a short name for the Buteyko method. Every word in the full name matters, but a literal reading without explanation often leads to mistakes.

What VEDB stands for

The name describes a person’s conscious work with disturbed breathing. In the school’s modern practical understanding, this is a voluntary effort to organize the measures, training sessions and exercises aimed at normalizing breathing.

What the word “voluntary” means

“Voluntary” does not mean that you have to clamp down on your breathing by force. Will shows itself in something else: a person decides to practice, follows the schedule, learns to relax, observes themselves and works with the factors that deepen breathing.

In other words, what is voluntary is above all the consciously organized process. It requires the person’s participation, but does not require constant muscular force.

What “elimination” means

What is eliminated is not breathing as such, but its excess depth. The reduction is carried out only to the physiological norm. The task is not to obtain the lowest minute volume at any cost, but to normalize respiratory and gas parameters.

What “deep breathing” in the name means

In the school’s terminology, deep breathing is hyperventilation, that is, lung ventilation that is excessive relative to the body’s current metabolic needs. So it is not only about an outwardly big inhalation. Excess breathing may look quiet and habitual.

Why VEDB does not mean suppressing breathing

If a person tenses up, endures a severe air shortage and then takes a big recovery breath, they are not following the basic logic of the method. The depth of inhalation should be reduced primarily through relaxation.

Historically, the concept of VEDB could include physically preventing an excessively deep inhalation, when a person was breathing markedly deeply and could not stop this breathing. In a chronic disorder without such obvious breathing, the main meaning of the voluntary effort is to organize relaxation and other actions that reduce the excess depth of inhalation.

How the name is connected with the practice of the method

The practice includes theoretical understanding, observation, relaxation, exercises, homework, monitoring of changes over time and work with factors that deepen breathing. All these actions are consciously organized and directed toward one goal: the normalization of breathing.

Common mistakes in understanding the term

VEDB is mistakenly imagined as constant breath holds, a ban on deep inhalation in any situation or a striving to breathe as infrequently as possible. Sometimes the word “voluntary” is understood as an order to endure, and “elimination” as the need to remove the normal increase in breathing even during physical exertion.

These interpretations miss the main point: only the excess is removed, only down to the norm and mainly through relaxation.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous.
