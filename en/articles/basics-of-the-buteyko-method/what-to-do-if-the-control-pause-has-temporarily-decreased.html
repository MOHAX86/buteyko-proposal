What to do if the control pause has temporarily decreased

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Do not draw a conclusion from one measurement and do not try to get the number back by increasing the load: check your technique, the circumstances and how you feel. If the decrease recurs, contact an instructor, and in the case of new, marked or alarming symptoms, a doctor.

First, do not draw a conclusion from one measurement

A single pause may differ because of the conditions or the technique.

Check your technique

Recall whether the exhalation was ordinary and whether your breathing remained calm after the hold.

Repeat the observation at the usual time, not immediately for the sake of a better number

A series of repeats in a row turns the measurement into a chase for a result. Return to the next scheduled CP.

Recall the circumstances of the past 24 hours

Sleep, stress, fatigue, physical load, food, stimulants and a change in routine provide important context.

Sleep, stress and fatigue

Note them in your diary without trying to prove the cause in advance.

Physical exercise, food and stimulants

Do not compare a measurement taken right after their effect with your usual morning CP.

Onset of feeling unwell or a change in routine

Write down the new circumstances and watch your condition.

Assess your well-being and symptoms

The CP is considered together with your familiar symptoms and general condition.

When a decrease may be a temporary fluctuation

If it is isolated, the conditions were different and your condition has not worsened, calm observation is enough.

When a combination of signs requires attention

A recurring decrease together with intensified symptoms needs to be discussed.

How to practice on such a day

Do not intensify the air shortage and do not try to compensate for the number with hard training.

Do not intensify the air shortage

Strenuous endurance and marked suffocation are considered a mistake.

Reduce the load and return to relaxation

Work only within the recommendations you have already mastered; if in doubt, take a break and contact your instructor.

Do not try to get the previous number back immediately

The task is to restore the quality of practice, not to beat the stopwatch.

When to contact an instructor

If the decrease recurs, the exercise worsens your condition or you have doubts about your technique, give your diary to your instructor.

A recurring decrease

The sequence of values and the context are important.

Worsening after an exercise

Stop the excessive load and ask for correction.

When medical help is needed

New, marked or alarming symptoms require medical assessment. Do not stop prescribed medicines on your own.

New, marked or alarming symptoms

Do not rely on breathing practice alone if there is noticeable worsening.

A short action plan

Check your technique, write down the circumstances, assess your condition, ease the practice, wait for the next comparable measurement, and contact an instructor or a doctor if the problem persists.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
