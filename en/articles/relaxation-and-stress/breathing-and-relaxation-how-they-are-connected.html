Breathing and relaxation: how they are connected

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Breathing responds to the state of the body. The Buteyko school considers relaxation the main means of reducing the excess depth of breathing to the norm.

The connection between breathing and relaxation

During a tense conversation or after alarming news, a person may find that their shoulders are raised, their abdomen is tense and their breaths have become more noticeable. Breathing responds to the state of the body. At the same time, it is impossible to establish from a single sensation how much lung ventilation has changed: both the depth and the rate of breathing matter.

What relaxation means in the Buteyko method

In the methodological position of the Buteyko school, the work is aimed at reducing the excess depth of breathing to the physiological norm. Relaxation is considered the main means, and the air shortage during a session remains slight. The voluntary effort consists in organizing the conditions for practice and gradually removing unnecessary tension, not in holding back the inhalation by force or enduring suffocation.

What you can notice in everyday life

Pay attention to your face, jaw, shoulders and abdomen. Are you maintaining your posture at the cost of tension? Do you feel a desire to take a big “test” breath? Such observations help you discuss your habitual breathing pattern with an instructor. In themselves they are not a diagnosis.

How to tell that the practice remains gentle

A comfortable position, a relaxed face and the absence of a severe air shortage are reasonable guides. After a session, your condition should not noticeably worsen. The school additionally looks at well-being, symptoms and changes in the control pause measured in comparable conditions.

When a different approach is needed

Relaxation does not eliminate all causes of shortness of breath and anxiety. If severe or new chest pain, marked lack of air, fainting or other acute symptoms appear, medical care is needed. Recurring complaints should be discussed with a doctor; breathing practice is chosen individually.

Result

Breathing and relaxation are connected through the work of the muscles and the body’s response to load. The goal of the sessions is to gradually normalize excess breathing without forcing yourself.
