Relaxation mistakes in breathing exercises

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Common mistakes are a big “calming” breath, a breath hold as a competition, tension instead of relaxation and continuing the session despite worsening.

Too big a “calming” breath

When you want to inhale deeply for relief, air starts coming in at a greater volume. If the task is to reduce the excess depth of breathing, such a technique can lead away from it. It is better to return to a comfortable position and ordinary breathing, without squeezing out a result.

A breath hold as a competition

The control pause ends before strenuous willful endurance. Trying to hold out as long as possible confuses it with a different task and may leave behind a strong desire to inhale. Do not use the stopwatch as a measure of your own success.

Tension instead of relaxation

Clenched teeth, raised shoulders, a drawn-in abdomen and a motionless chest are not evidence of a correct exercise. There is no need to hold the diaphragm by force or to impose a patterned movement on the abdomen. The school links reducing the excess depth of inhalation primarily with relaxation.

Continuing despite worsening

If marked discomfort or dizziness appears, or your usual symptoms intensify, the session is stopped. It is important to tell the instructor what arose and when, so that the load can be changed.
