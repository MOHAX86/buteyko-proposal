Tension of the diaphragm: what a person may feel

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. A “clamped diaphragm” is what people often call tightness under the ribs, a feeling of an incomplete breath or tension in the upper abdomen. Such sensations are worth observing, without pushing through them by force or diagnosing yourself.

Why the sensations can be hard to name

A feeling of tightness under the ribs, of an incomplete breath or of tension in the upper part of the abdomen is often described as a “clamped diaphragm”. But from a description alone it is impossible to determine which muscle exactly is tense. Similar sensations arise for various reasons, including ones unrelated to breathing practice.

What to observe

Note when the discomfort appears: after eating, when agitated, during physical exertion or only when you deliberately try to control your inhalation. Body position, accompanying pain, cough, heartburn and changes in symptoms over time are relevant. This information is more useful for a conversation with a doctor than an attempt to diagnose yourself.

What to do during practice

Do not try to push through the resistance with a big inhalation, hold your abdomen or “lower the diaphragm” by force. Return to a comfortable position and to ordinary calm breathing. If the session causes or intensifies discomfort, stop and tell your instructor.

When to seek help

Sudden severe chest pain, marked shortness of breath, fainting or rapid worsening require urgent medical assessment. Persistent or recurring sensations should also be discussed with a doctor.
