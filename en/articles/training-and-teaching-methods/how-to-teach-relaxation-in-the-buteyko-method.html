How to teach relaxation in the Buteyko method

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Relaxation is taught through observation, precise prompts and checking sensations, so that the student releases unnecessary tension and does not control the inhalation by force.

In the approach of Alik Mullakhmetov’s school, relaxation is the main way of reducing excessive depth of breathing to the physiological norm. It is therefore important for the teacher to help the student recognize unnecessary tension and release it without forceful control of the inhalation.

Start with a clear observation

The general command “Relax completely” often does not explain to a person what to do. It is more useful to draw attention to specific areas: whether the hands are clenched, whether the face is tense, whether the arms are comfortably placed. At the same time, there is no need to look for tension everywhere and anxiously control every movement.

The instructor helps to choose a comfortable position. The back can be straight without being held rigidly, and the shoulders naturally lowered. A person does not need to deliberately pull them down or assume a model posture at the cost of new effort.

Relaxation without suppressing breathing

A student may try to carry out the task more forcefully: tensing the abdomen, holding the chest still or deliberately blocking the inhalation. Such actions need to be distinguished from reducing excess breathing through relaxation. Questions about sensations are important, not just outward stillness.

After a short review, the teacher asks the student to repeat the action and describe the difference. This helps to understand which explanation proved useful. If a new prompt causes even more tension, it is changed.

Transfer into everyday life

A mastered skill is not limited to the armchair. One can learn to notice it during familiar household activities or calm walking, when such an assignment has been agreed with the instructor. The goal is less unnecessary effort in ordinary life, not constant immobility.

Result

Relaxation is not the same as sleepiness or going limp; it helps to perform an action without unnecessary tension.
