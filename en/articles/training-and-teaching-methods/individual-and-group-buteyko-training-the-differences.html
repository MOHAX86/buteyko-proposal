Individual and group Buteyko training: the differences

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. The formats differ above all in how the teacher’s attention is distributed and how feedback is organized. Compare them by explanation, checking and correction.

The choice between individual and group training depends on how much a person needs detailed checking of their performance and how they master new material.

Individual lessons

At a one-on-one meeting, more time can be devoted to one student’s questions. The teacher observes the performance, asks about sensations and returns to whatever is unclear as many times as needed within the lesson. The pace is easier to match to the student’s preparation and emerging difficulties.

This format is especially convenient when a person finds it hard to describe their sensations, has persistent mistakes or needs a detailed check of practice already mastered. However, the label “individual training” in itself does not guarantee attention: what matters is the teacher’s actual work.

Group lessons

In a group, participants receive a common explanation, watch a demonstration and hear the answers to other people’s questions. Regular meetings help organize the studies. At the same time, you need to understand how the teacher checks each participant and which questions require separate discussion.

A group does not have to do everything in the same amount. A common topic can be combined with different assignments if the teacher knows how prepared the students are and explains the differences.

Online and recordings

Whether a lesson is in person or remote is another characteristic of the format. During an online meeting, it is important that the connection allows the teacher to observe the student and discuss the performance. A recording is useful for reviewing familiar material, but by itself it does not give the teacher an opportunity to see a mistake and ask a clarifying question.
