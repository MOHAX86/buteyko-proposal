How to explain the control pause to a beginner

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Explain that the control pause is a measurement, not a maximum breath hold: it ends at the first desire to inhale, and breathing afterwards should remain practically the same as before the pause.

When explaining the control pause, it is useful to start with a definition: in the school’s methodological base, the CP is the duration of a breath hold at the end of an ordinary, unaccentuated exhalation after several minutes of rest.

First, the meaning of the measurement

It is useful to explain to a beginner why records are kept and why the conditions should be comparable. The task is to obtain an observation for further work with the instructor. The urge to show more seconds changes the meaning of the measurement and makes it harder to assess progress over time.

For the first explanation, the guideline “until the first desire to inhale” is suitable. The student should not move on to hard endurance and pronounced voluntary effort. After the pause ends, the breathing pattern should remain practically the same as before it, with no need to take a large recovery breath.

Show and check understanding

The verbal explanation is supplemented with a demonstration, and then the student is asked to show the action under observation. It is important to find out what kind of exhalation they made before the pause, by what sensation they ended it and how they breathed afterwards. Without this information, the number of seconds says little about whether it was done correctly.

If a person inhales deeply beforehand, exhales with effort or continues holding the breath for the sake of a record, it is necessary to return to the explanation. A duration obtained at the price of incorrect technique should not be praised.

How to discuss the records

Measurements taken under similar conditions are compared, and the circumstances are taken into account. A single result is not a diagnosis or a final assessment of how well the method has been mastered. What matters more is that the conditions are repeatable and that the student understands what they are measuring.

Result

It is better to learn this for the first time with an instructor who can see the performance and correct a mistake.
