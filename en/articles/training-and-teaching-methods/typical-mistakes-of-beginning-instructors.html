Typical mistakes of beginning instructors

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Typical mistakes are too much material at once, the same assignments for everyone, chasing indicators, no checking and an accusatory tone.

A novice instructor may know the theory well and still have difficulty teaching. A common reason is focusing on their own explanation instead of checking what the student has understood and is doing.

Too much material at once

The wish to tell everything in a single meeting overloads a beginner. They memorize separate phrases and try to connect them on their own. It is better to choose the next learning task, explain it and check the performance. The remaining questions can be covered as the student progresses.

The same assignments for everyone

The same explanation is not necessarily equally clear to different people. It is important for the teacher to take into account experience, pace of learning and actual performance. Adjusting an assignment is a normal part of training, not a sign of an unsuccessful student.

Chasing indicators

If the teacher praises only big numbers, the student may start to endure, hide discomfort and try to beat the previous result. It is more useful to discuss the measurement conditions and the quality of the skill. The control pause should not turn into a maximum breath hold for the sake of approval.

No checking and feedback

The phrase “Keep practicing” does not help if a mistake goes unnoticed. It is necessary to see the habitual performance, ask about sensations and give a specific prompt. Then check whether it helped.

Another mistake is talking about difficulties in an accusatory tone. The student should be able to say honestly that they did not understand an assignment or were unable to complete it. The school’s materials emphasize patience, accuracy of terms and the instructor’s own continuous learning.

Result

An instructor’s professional growth includes reviewing their own work. It is useful to discuss difficult cases with experienced colleagues, improve explanations and check what the student has actually learned.
