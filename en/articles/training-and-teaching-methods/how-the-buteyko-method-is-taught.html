How the Buteyko method is taught

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Training proceeds step by step under the instructor’s supervision: explanation, demonstration, independent performance and checking of the skill.

Teaching the Buteyko method in the approach of Alik Mullakhmetov’s school is aimed at normalizing breathing mainly through relaxation and removing the factors that deepen it. The student’s task is to understand the principle and learn to apply it without forcibly suppressing breathing.

From explanation to personal experience

At the beginning, the instructor finds out what request the person has come with, what they already know and how they practiced before. The instructor then explains the basic concepts: what the school means by deep breathing, what the control pause is used for and what role relaxation plays. Theory is needed for meaningful practice: the student should understand what exactly they are doing and by what signs they assess their performance.

The next step is a demonstration and a trial attempt. The instructor observes, notices unnecessary tension and asks about sensations. The simple question “Is everything clear?” is not enough. It is more useful to ask the person to explain the task in their own words and show it once more.

Why feedback is needed

At home, the student carries out the assignment that has already been gone through and writes down observations. At the next meeting, the teacher checks the performance, discusses difficulties and changes the assignment if necessary. In this way, training becomes consistent work rather than a set of unconnected exercises.

Mastering the method does not come down to lengthening the breath hold. What matters is understanding, quality of performance, the ability to notice tension and the organization of everyday practice. In this approach, willpower is needed above all for consistent work, while excessive depth of breathing is reduced mainly through relaxation.
