Typical mistakes when doing Buteyko exercises

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. The most common mistakes are fighting your breathing instead of relaxing, chasing seconds, or sharply increasing the load. Correction begins with relaxation and reducing the load.

Most mistakes arise when a person changes the goal of the practice. This is not a reason to feel ashamed but a signal to return to the criteria of correct performance.

Mistakes in understanding the goal

The main mistake is to think that the smaller the breathing and the longer the breath hold, the better.

Breathing as little as possible

The method aims at normalization to the physiological norm, not at minimizing breathing.

Chasing seconds and results

A control pause record achieved by endurance does not prove that the exercise was done correctly.

Preparation mistakes

Rushing, recent exertion, and an uncomfortable posture make it hard to see your baseline breathing.

Starting in a hurry or after exertion

First you need to recover and sit quietly for a few minutes.

Uncomfortable position and hidden tension

Raised shoulders, a pulled-in abdomen, and a stiff back create muscular restraint.

Mistakes during the exercise

The most dangerous are forcibly reducing inhalation, an excessive air shortage, and continuing despite deterioration.

Muscular restraint of inhalation

A person may clamp the chest, abdomen, or throat. Outwardly the inhalation becomes smaller, but there is no relaxation.

Excessive air shortage

Strong suffocation is neither a necessary nor a useful stage.

Continuing despite deterioration

Worsening symptoms, dizziness, panic, and feeling markedly unwell require stopping the load.

Mistakes in finishing

An abrupt exit and repetitions for the sake of a better result increase the load.

Deep recovery breaths

They show that the exercise was excessive or was finished incorrectly.

Immediate repetition for the sake of a better result

Let your condition recover fully and do not turn the session into a series of tests.

Routine mistakes

Too great a volume and irregularity equally hinder the formation of the skill.

Too great a load

Do not add time or intensity on your own.

Irregularity and changing the assignment on your own

Frequently changing exercises makes it impossible to understand your response and consolidate the skill.

How to correct mistakes

The first step is to reduce the load and restore relaxation.

Make the exercise shorter and gentler only within the limits of the recommendations. In case of marked discomfort, stop.

Discuss your technique with your instructor

Pass on your notes and accurately describe your sensations, recovery, and symptoms. The instructor will correct your performance.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
