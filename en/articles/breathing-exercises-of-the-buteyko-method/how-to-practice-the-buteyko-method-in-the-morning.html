How to practice the Buteyko method in the morning

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. In the morning, first take a few minutes of rest and measure the morning control pause (MCP), then do only an exercise you have mastered with your instructor. What matters is relaxation and only a slight air shortage, without chasing records.

Morning practice lets you assess your baseline condition before the active events of the day and calmly begin working with your breathing.

The purpose of morning practice

In the morning, it is important to get a comparable observation and complete the prescribed load without rushing.

Baseline observation before the events of the day

Food, coffee, physical activity, and emotions change the conditions. That is why the MCP is measured before these influences, as far as your routine allows.

A few minutes of silence help separate the measurement from the bustle after waking.

Follow one habitual order. Sit comfortably, relax, and breathe as usual for a few minutes.

The same sequence

If today the CP is measured right after waking, and tomorrow after exercise and coffee, the comparison will be weak.

Do not try to reduce your breathing beforehand to get a better MCP.

The morning control pause

The MCP is performed after a normal, gentle exhalation and lasts until the first distinct signs of a need to inhale.

The baseline value is recorded before practice. It belongs to the morning series, not to checking the effect of a particular exercise.

Why you should not strive for a better number

Deliberately prolonging the pause makes the result not comparable. What matters is using the same technique, not setting a record.

The main part of the session

Do the exercise, for the duration, that your instructor has prescribed.

Do not add new techniques or increase the time just because you feel better in the morning.

Relaxation and moderation

Keep the air shortage relatively comfortable. The morning session should not exhaust you before the day begins.

Checking your response after practice

After a calm recovery, you can assess your CP, familiar symptoms, and well-being.

A positive response is judged by the three criteria taken together.

A short diary entry

Write down your MCP, the practice, your response, and any unusual circumstances.

What can change the morning result

Lack of sleep, rushing, stress, the onset of feeling unwell, and unaccustomed activity make a particular morning special.

Do not try to correct a lowered value by intensifying the exercise. Note the context.

When the morning session needs to be made easier

In case of marked fatigue, feeling unwell, or an adverse reaction, reduce the load within the limits of the recommendations and contact your instructor. New alarming symptoms require medical assessment.

Conclusion

Morning practice is a calm, repeatable system: rest, the MCP, a mastered exercise, recovery, and a short note.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
