How to combine Buteyko exercises with ordinary physical activity

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Practice and movement are combined by keeping their tasks separate: at rest you learn relaxation and observation, and during exertion you do not suppress the necessary increase in breathing. After exertion, you let it recover calmly.

Breathing practice and ordinary physical activity serve different but compatible purposes. Exercise teaches observation, relaxation, and reducing excess ventilation. Movement increases the body’s need for gas exchange, so during exertion you must not forcibly suppress the necessary increase in breathing.

Why keep up ordinary physical activity

Movement remains part of everyday life and supports your ability to carry out ordinary tasks. The amount of activity is chosen according to your condition and medical restrictions.

Walking, household chores, and manageable physical exercise provide a natural load that should not be set against breathing practice.

Taking your individual condition into account

In case of illness, recovery after illness, or pronounced symptoms, your activity routine should be agreed with a doctor.

Exercise and movement have different purposes

The sensations of quiet seated practice cannot be mechanically transferred to active movement.

At rest, a person learns to notice unnecessary effort and return to calm breathing.

Working muscles require increased ventilation. This in itself is not a mistake.

When to do breathing practice

It is better to separate the main exercise from intense activity with a period of calm recovery.

Do not start right after intense exertion

While breathing is naturally increased, trying to reduce it sharply can cause discomfort.

Start practicing when you can sit quietly and your breathing has come close to its usual level.

How to observe your breathing during activity

Watch not for the smallest volume of air, but for the absence of needless tension.

Do not suppress the necessary increase in ventilation

Do not endure strong suffocation, and do not keep moving on a long breath hold unless this has been specifically prescribed.

Where possible, keep inhaling through the nose at a suitable intensity

Nasal inhalation can serve as a guide to manageable activity if it is unobstructed and causes no marked discomfort. At higher exertion, breathing changes naturally.

How to recover after exertion

Slow your pace, relax your shoulders, and gradually return to calm breathing.

Do not try to stop the increased breathing instantly by force.

After recovery, assess your condition. Do not use the maximum pause as a mandatory test after exertion.

How to distribute the load over the day

Do not pile several unaccustomed loads into one day, and consider the timing of your breathing sessions.

A sharp simultaneous increase in physical and breathing work makes the response harder to understand.

Agree changes with your instructor and doctor

The instructor corrects the breathing practice; the doctor sets medical limits on physical activity.

When to reduce activity or stop

Slow down or stop moving if you have marked discomfort, unusual shortness of breath, pain, dizziness, or a worsening condition. New alarming symptoms require medical assessment.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
