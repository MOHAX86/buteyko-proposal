How to do the breathing relaxation exercise

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Sit calmly, observe your breathing, relax your face, shoulders, chest, and abdomen, and gently reduce the effort of inhaling until you feel a slight air shortage. Your technique needs to be checked with an instructor.

The exercise is done in a calm and safe place. Its task is to remove unnecessary muscle effort and allow breathing to become less deep. Below is a general sequence that needs to be clarified and checked with an instructor.

Preparing the place and position

Set aside a few minutes when you will not be distracted. You must not practice where a change in attention could be dangerous: while driving, in water, or while operating machinery.

Sit so that your feet and pelvis have stable support. Put your phone away and give yourself time so that you do not have to rush.

Your back stays straight but not tense. Your head is not tipped back, your hands rest on your knees, and your abdomen is free.

Starting the exercise

The first few minutes are not for reducing your breathing but for observing it.

Do not deliberately deepen or slow your breathing. Let it show itself as it is right now.

Notice the movements of your chest and abdomen, how active your inhalation is, and any tension in your face and shoulders. Do not try to correct everything at once.

Relaxation sequence

Move from clearly noticeable muscles to those involved in breathing.

Face and jaw

Smooth your forehead and relax your eyelids, tongue, and jaw. Your lips stay gently closed if nasal breathing is unobstructed.

Shoulders and arms

Let your shoulders drop and your hands rest quietly on your knees. Do not force your shoulders down.

Chest and abdomen

Give up deliberately lifting your chest and pulling in your abdomen. Keep the natural small movements.

Reducing the effort of inhalation

Gently decrease the muscular involvement in inhalation. Do not cut it off and do not block your airways.

How to maintain a moderate load

Be guided by your ability to stay relaxed. A slight air shortage is acceptable; a severe one is not. The sensation remains relatively comfortable and does not require a constant struggle.

When to ease off the exercise immediately

In case of strong suffocation, dizziness, panic, worsening symptoms, or sharp deterioration, stop reducing your breathing and return to natural, calm breathing.

Finishing the practice

Gradually let go of control and sit quietly for a few minutes.

Do not deliberately take deep breaths. If they occur on their own and are pronounced, tell your instructor.

Checking the result

After full recovery, assess your control pause, familiar symptoms, and general condition. Good practice should not leave you feeling worse.

It is enough to note the duration, your CP before and after, changes in symptoms and well-being, and any unusual circumstances.

When an instructor’s correction is needed

Correction is needed if you do not recognize the point of moderate load, keep tensing up, take recovery breaths, or notice that you feel worse.

Brief instructions

Prepare → observe → relax your face, shoulders, chest, and abdomen → reduce the effort of inhalation → keep it moderate → finish gently → assess the response.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
