How to practice the Buteyko method in the evening

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. In the evening, you calmly do an exercise you have already mastered, taking fatigue into account: you relax and reduce unnecessary effort in inhalation. Records and a direct comparison with the morning control pause (MCP) are not needed.

By evening, your breathing has already been affected by the events of the day, food, physical activity, and emotions. These are taken into account along with fatigue and your current condition.

How evening practice differs from morning practice

The morning control pause (MCP) serves as the main series of long-term observations. Evening practice reflects your condition after the day and serves the purpose of relaxing and maintaining the skill.

Evening results may differ even when progress is stable. This is not a reason to immediately intensify your practice.

The goal is to calm down, not to set a record

Do not prolong pauses or create a severe air shortage just to feel that you have done the work.

Preparing for the evening session

Set aside time after your main tasks are done, when you can sit quietly.

A break after eating and activity

Do not start right after a heavy meal or intense exertion. Let your breathing and well-being return to their usual state.

A quiet place and a comfortable posture

Remove bright distractions, sit in a stable position, and loosen your clothing if it presses on your abdomen.

How to choose the evening load

Use only the prescribed range and take your condition into account.

Take fatigue and agitation into account

On a tiring day, a gentler practice may be more appropriate than the usual one.

Use only exercises you have mastered

Evening is not the time to try out a new intensive technique on your own.

How to do the evening practice

Begin with observation and general relaxation, then reduce unnecessary effort in inhalation.

Relax your face, shoulders, chest, and abdomen.

Keep the air shortage moderate

If the sensation becomes severe, reduce the load at once.

How to finish the session

Gradually let go of controlling your breathing and sit quietly.

Do not leave yourself with marked agitation or suffocation

Correct practice should not require a long recovery.

Write down your condition if necessary

Note any unusual reaction, symptoms, and circumstances if they are important for your instructor.

When to shorten or postpone practice

Shorten or postpone the session if you feel markedly unwell or very tired, if conditions are unsafe, or if you feel worse during the exercise.

Evening practice and sleep

Relaxation may help you move into a calmer state, but this article does not promise treatment of sleep disorders. If the practice agitates you or makes you feel worse, its timing and load should be reconsidered.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
