A short 5-minute breathing practice

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. In five minutes you can pause, notice your breathing, relax unnecessary effort, and calmly finish a practice you have mastered. This supports the skill but does not replace training.

A short format can support an already mastered skill on a busy day. It does not replace full training or the individually prescribed amount of practice.

When a short format is useful

A short practice is suitable as a maintenance option when a full session is impossible or when you need to return to observation after a tense episode.

It is better to do a familiar action in moderation than to make up for a missed session later with an excessive load.

Five minutes help you notice whether any unnecessary tension has remained after your activities.

What to prepare in a few seconds

Find a safe place where you do not need to move, watch the road, or operate equipment.

Sit in a stable position, place your feet on the floor, and rest your hands on your knees.

No practice on the move or in a dangerous situation

Do not reduce your breathing or do breath holds while driving, in water, at height, or during work that requires constant attention.

First minute: pause and observe

Breathe as usual. Notice your face, shoulders, chest, abdomen, and the effort of inhalation.

Second and third minutes: relax your breathing

Gradually reduce unnecessary muscular involvement, step by step.

Do not clamp your breathing; instead, let the inhalation become calmer as you relax.

Only a relatively comfortable air shortage is acceptable.

Fourth minute: maintain a calm pattern

Keep the relaxation you have found without trying to reduce your breathing even further every second.

Fifth minute: finish gently and assess your condition

Let go of the exercise, breathe calmly, and notice how your symptoms and well-being have changed.

What five minutes can and cannot give you

The short format supports regularity but does not replace the main program.

It reminds your body and attention of a familiar way to relax.

In five minutes you cannot accomplish every task, check a complex technique, or safely increase the load.

When to stop the short practice

Stop in case of strong suffocation, dizziness, panic, worsening symptoms, or a worsening condition. Return to natural breathing, and if it happens again, discuss the episode with your instructor.

Reminder

1 minute of observation → 2 minutes of gradual relaxation → 1 minute of calm maintenance → 1 minute of gentle finishing. Do only a version you have mastered.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
