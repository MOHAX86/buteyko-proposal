Breathing during strength exercises: main principles

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Do not hold your breath or strain unless necessary. If you cannot manage the movement otherwise, choose an easier option.

In strength exercises, attention is usually occupied by the movement: how to stand up, lift an object or hold a position. Against this background a person may imperceptibly stop breathing and start straining. For everyday health-oriented exercise, it is important not to add such effort unless necessary.

Why the breath gets held

The wish to complete a difficult repetition sometimes leads a person to first take in air and then tense up the whole body. This can happen not only with a heavy barbell, but also when getting up from a low chair or carrying an awkward bag.

Straining while holding the breath can sharply raise blood pressure and cause dizziness. ACSM specifically draws attention to this risk during strength work in people with hypertension. So such a habit should not be considered a harmless way to finish a set.

First make the exercise manageable

If a movement forces you every time to hold your breath and strain to the limit, it is useful to reconsider the weight, the number of repetitions or the version of the exercise itself. A trainer will help to choose an easier form and check your technique.

You cannot demand free breathing of yourself while keeping a task that is clearly beyond your strength. For example, standing up from a higher chair is sometimes wiser than repeatedly overcoming a low seat by straining. The specific option depends on your condition and the goal of the sessions.

Is there a universal rule for inhaling and exhaling

Many ordinary exercises use exhalation during the effort, but prescribing one pattern for all movements is wrong. Body position, technique and the nature of the work matter. Some sports techniques require special training and are not automatically carried over into health-oriented exercise.

For a beginner, a clearer task is not to stop breathing involuntarily and not to take big preparatory breaths unless necessary. The details of breathing practice are agreed with an instructor, and the movement with a trainer.

Relaxation without losing support

Working muscles must do their job. Relaxation does not mean letting go of an object or losing stability. What is removed is the unnecessary tension in the face, jaws and shoulders that does not help the movement.

In case of dizziness, pain or marked worsening, the exercise is stopped.
