How to use walking in breathing practice

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Choose one thing to observe on your walk, for example the position of your shoulders. Do not add breath holds on your own.

A walk can be made part of work on habits without turning it into a separate complicated training session. Its advantage is the chance to observe yourself in a familiar activity: on the way to the shop, in the yard or on your usual route. At the same time, your attention should remain on the road and on the people around you.

Define one task

Before going out, choose what you want to observe. For example, whether your shoulders rise when you speed up or whether a big preparatory breath appears before a slope. One specific task is enough. Constantly checking your face, belly, stride and every second of breathing will only make the walk harder.

Do not set yourself a mandatory result in the form of a pause or a distance covered. To begin with, it is useful simply to discover a recurring habit. This is material for further work, not a reason to reproach yourself.

Build the observation into your route

On a safe level stretch, briefly pay attention to the chosen area, then return to ordinary walking. If your shoulders are tense, release the unneeded effort without disturbing the position of your trunk. If you are in a hurry, make your pace more comfortable. There is no need to force yourself to breathe less often: in the school, excessive depth of breathing is reduced primarily by relaxation and only to normal.

Observation does not require closing your eyes, slowing your reaction to those around you or keeping a continuous count. At crossings, on stairs and on uneven ground, safety of movement is more important. Breathing practice must not distract from it.

Why you should not add breath holds on your own

Walking is already a physical task. Pauses to the limit, competing in steps taken without inhaling and attempts to endure until dizziness change the nature of the session. They cannot be considered a mandatory continuation of a calm walk.

If an instructor prescribes exercises in motion, they explain their purpose, conditions and stopping criteria. A general article cannot replace such tailoring. In the methodological framework of the school, learning VEDB on your own without feedback is not recommended.

What to write down after a walk

A few observations are enough: the chosen route, how well you tolerated it, the moment tension appeared and whether slowing down helped. There is no need to record every breath in detail. The notes should make the conversation with your instructor easier, not become a new burden.

Walking is useful for practice above all because it is repeatable. A small, clear task that you manage to do regularly is more practical than a complicated scheme that quickly becomes tiring.
