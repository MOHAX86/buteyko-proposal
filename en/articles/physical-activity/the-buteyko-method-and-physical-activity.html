The Buteyko method and physical activity

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Manageable movement is part of the work of normalizing breathing. It should not turn into an endurance test or an attempt to breathe less often at any cost.

A manageable load makes it possible to carry relaxation skills into ordinary life: a walk, housework, exercise.

Why breathing changes during movement

During a walk and during exercises, breathing may become more noticeable than at rest. This in itself does not mean a mistake. You cannot demand of a working body the same breathing as during rest. In a 1972 lecture, Buteyko specifically pointed out the need to consider breathing in connection with metabolism, rather than judging only the outward depth of the inhalation.

So the task of the method is to normalize breathing that is excessive relative to the body’s needs. It differs from striving to reduce every inhalation regardless of circumstances. In Alik Mullakhmetov’s school, the main path of this work is linked with relaxation and the removal of factors that deepen breathing.

How to combine movement and relaxation

Relaxation while walking does not mean sluggishness or loss of support. The legs keep working, the trunk keeps a comfortable position. What is removed is extra effort: clenched jaws, raised shoulders, tense hands, the habit of taking in more air in advance. It is useful to notice whether ordinary movement is accompanied by needless haste.

It is easier to start with a familiar activity. If a walk already demands a struggle for every step, you should not add self-directed breathing restriction to it. First a lighter load is chosen, then you decide together with an instructor which elements of the practice are appropriate.

Nasal breathing and choosing the load

In the everyday practice of the school, nasal breathing serves as an important guide. With a clear nose and a manageable pace, it is easier to maintain without tension. If that requires pressing your lips together and enduring a marked air shortage, the load needs to be made lighter. The rule should not turn into a competition with your own body.

Running, strength exercises and a calm walk make different demands. One pattern cannot be carried over to every activity. A trainer helps with movement technique, an instructor with the details of VEDB, and a doctor determines the restrictions in case of illness.

How to assess the result

What matters is not a single successful episode, but how well regular activity is tolerated. Note how the walk went, when tension appeared and what changed after rest. The control pause is compared under identical conditions of rest, not measured on the move to check for success.

If the load causes new or increasing symptoms, it is reviewed. Breathing practice does not replace prescribed treatment. The main guide is movement that can be repeated without forcing yourself and gradually built into an ordinary day.
