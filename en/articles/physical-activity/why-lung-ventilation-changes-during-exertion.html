Why lung ventilation changes during exertion

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. During movement, the respiratory system adapts to a new task. An increase in ventilation that matches the demand during exertion is not hyperventilation.

Lung ventilation is the movement of air into and out of the lungs. Its minute volume depends on how much air passes in one breathing cycle and how many such cycles occur per minute. That is why breathing rate alone is not enough to assess its volume.

How the body adapts to movement

The transition from rest to work is accompanied by a change in breathing. Not only chemical values but also nerve signals connected with the start of movement and muscle work take part in the regulation. The respiratory system adapts to the changed task.

In textbook physiology, an increase in ventilation that matches the demand during exertion is distinguished from hyperventilation, which is ventilation excessive relative to that demand. So the phrases “breathing has increased” and “hyperventilation has occurred” are not equivalent.

Why appearances can deceive

Two people may take the same number of breaths, but of different depth. Or one person breathes more often during a climb and less often after stopping. Without considering the work being done, it cannot be decided which pattern is excessive.

You should not diagnose yourself because your chest has started to move more noticeably. Conversely, infrequent breathing in itself does not confirm that ventilation matches the demand. It is important not to turn observation into a search for a single outward sign of correctness.

What is changed in Buteyko practice

According to the methodological framework of the school, the work is directed at excessive depth of breathing and factors that deepen breathing. For example, haste, emotional tension or a habit of taking in air in advance may be added to the physical task itself.

A beginner is not obliged to calculate on their own what share of breathing is necessary and what share is excess. It is more practically useful to choose a manageable load, remove unneeded muscular effort and discuss your observations with an instructor. Trying to maintain resting breathing during hard work replaces this task with a different one.

How to use this understanding

Compare similar situations: the same stretch of road, a close pace, a similar state of well-being. Note changes in tolerance, not only the number of breaths. If a habitual activity has become unexpectedly difficult, the cause should be investigated, without automatically putting it down to deep breathing.
