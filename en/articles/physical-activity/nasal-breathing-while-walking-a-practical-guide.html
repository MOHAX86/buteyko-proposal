Nasal breathing while walking: a practical guide

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Walk at a comfortable pace and, with a clear nose, breathe through it as far as you can without a struggle. If breathing becomes laborious, slow down or rest.

Ordinary walking helps to notice breathing habits that are hard to detect while sitting. Some start hurrying as soon as they leave home, others raise their shoulders on every slope or take a big breath before a conversation. The task of observation is to see such moments and make the walk more comfortable.

Prepare suitable conditions

Choose a familiar level stretch where you do not have to keep going around obstacles or hurry to a crossing. Your clothes should not restrict movement. If your nose is markedly blocked, do not try to overcome this by effort: the question of clearing it is dealt with separately.

Before starting, there is no need to deliberately “breathe your fill,” check your maximum pause or take in air in reserve. Begin with an ordinary comfortable pace. Such a walk does not require a preset distance or a mandatory speed.

Observe without constant control

With a clear nose, keep up nasal breathing as far as this works without a struggle. From time to time, note the position of your shoulders, face and hands. There is no need to control every breath or deliberately keep your chest still.

It is more useful to briefly notice tension and release it than to watch yourself the whole way. For example, if on meeting an acquaintance you sped up and clenched your jaws, first return to a comfortable pace. Do not try to keep the former speed and forcibly reduce the inhalation at the same time.

What to do if you are uncomfortable

If breathing becomes laborious, reduce your speed. If necessary, stop and rest. You should not carry on just to complete a planned loop with your mouth closed. Being able to breathe through the nose should not be paid for with marked discomfort.

Counting steps per inhalation and exhalation sometimes appeals by its simplicity, but there is no universal ratio for all people and routes. This walk needs neither a mandatory rhythm nor breath holds to the limit. Individual exercises in motion are learned with an instructor.

After the walk

Recall on which stretch it became harder: when speeding up, on a slope, during a conversation. Such details will help to choose the next load more precisely. If the walk went easily, this does not oblige you to increase the distance right away.
