Hyperventilation in the scientific literature

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Hyperventilation is ventilation of the lungs that is excessive relative to current metabolic needs: more CO₂ is removed than is required for the normal gas balance.

Hyperventilation is an important topic in respiratory physiology and one of the central topics of the Buteyko method.

The main point of the work: to bring breathing into line with the needs of the body.

Why the volume of breathing matters

Both the rate and the depth of breathing affect ventilation. A person may breathe noticeably more often or increase the volume of individual inhalations. The Buteyko school pays special attention to excessive depth of inhalation and the factors that intensify it.

Physiology links the CO₂ level to the ratio between its production in metabolism and its removal through the lungs. Therefore breathing is assessed in the context of the person’s state: rest, movement, exertion or recovery.

How CO₂ is connected with well-being

A change in the carbon dioxide level affects the acid-base balance and the regulation of blood flow. Pronounced excessive removal of CO₂ may be accompanied by dizziness, tingling, a feeling of faintness and other unpleasant manifestations.

Studies of breathing disorders also consider tension of the respiratory muscles, movement of the chest and the subjective feeling of air shortage. Such attention to several sides of breathing helps to work with complaints in a meaningful way.

What the Buteyko method offers

In the methodological position of Alik Mullakhmetov’s Health Academy, the work is aimed at reducing excessive breathing to the physiological norm. The main path is relaxation and the step-by-step elimination of factors that deepen breathing.

A person learns to notice tension, unnecessary deepening of the inhalation and the circumstances in which breathing changes. Then, together with an instructor, they build a practice that suits their condition.

Why understand the mechanism

Knowledge about hyperventilation explains why more air does not always mean more suitable breathing.

This knowledge helps to understand the purpose of relaxation, of dosing physical load and of observing how you feel. The practical work is learned with an instructor, with the new skills gradually carried over into everyday life.
