Nasal breathing in scientific research

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. The nose warms, humidifies and cleans the air. Researchers study nasal breathing in connection with gas exchange, physical exertion, vascular responses and nitric oxide.

Nasal breathing performs several physiological tasks: air passes through the nasal passages and is warmed, humidified and cleaned. The structure of the nose takes part in organizing the airflow, and the paranasal sinuses are connected with the formation of nitric oxide.

The nose as part of the respiratory system

The passage of air through the nose helps to prepare it for entering the lower airways. The mucous membrane and the structural features of the nasal passages create the conditions for this processing.

In the Buteyko school, nasal breathing receives attention as an everyday skill. It is considered together with relaxation, the volume of ventilation and the person’s condition.

This approach helps to make the breathing skill part of ordinary life: during rest, conversation, walks and recovery after exertion.

What researchers study

Physiological studies compare nasal and mouth breathing under specific conditions. They assess ventilation, heart rate, gas exchange and other measures.

Nitric oxide, which passes from the area of the nose and sinuses into the airways, is of particular interest. In a clinical experiment in patients after heart surgery, researchers observed a change in pulmonary vascular resistance when the route of inhalation was switched.

Exercise studies examine how the way of breathing is related to the physiological response to work.

How this is connected with the Buteyko method

In the school, nasal breathing is viewed as part of the overall normalization of breathing. It is combined with reducing unnecessary breathing effort and working with factors that deepen the inhalation.

What matters is a comfortable skill that a person can maintain in conditions suitable for them. With persistent nasal congestion, it is necessary to look into its cause and restore the airway, rather than try to force yourself to keep up an unsuitable way of breathing.

The practical meaning of the scientific base

Research shows that the route the air takes has physiological significance.

This knowledge helps to understand why training pays attention to nasal breathing. It is better to master the skill and carry it over to exertion gradually, taking into account how you feel and the instructor’s recommendations.
