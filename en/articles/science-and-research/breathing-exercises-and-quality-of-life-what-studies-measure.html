Breathing exercises and quality of life: what studies measure

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Questionnaire-based studies assess symptoms, activity limitations, emotional experiences and the influence of the environment, both before training and after it.

Quality of life shows how freely a person can go about ordinary activities. For someone with breathing complaints, this means being able to walk a familiar route, talk without constantly paying attention to breathing, get enough sleep and take part in family life.

That is why studies of breathing methods include questions about well-being and daily activity. Such results help to see the benefit of training through the eyes of the participants themselves.

What researchers assess

Quality-of-life questionnaires cover different sides of a person’s condition: symptoms, activity limitations, emotional experiences and the influence of the environment. The participant answers the same questions before training and after it.

If a person gives up usual activities because of breathing less often, worries less about symptoms or copes more easily with ordinary activity, this is reflected in the answers.

Standardized questionnaires turn personal experience into data that can be compared across many participants.

What results have been obtained for Buteyko

Studies of Buteyko training assessed quality of life, symptoms and asthma control. An improvement in quality of life was noted; the positive changes appeared mainly in symptoms or in the use of medicines.

Everyday well-being and examination results complement each other. A person feels more freedom in ordinary life thanks to fewer troubling symptoms and better control of their condition.

What matters in everyday practice

Specific observations from the student are useful to the instructor: how they sleep, cope with walks, feel after work, and recover after agitation. At the school, these observations are considered together with the control pause and general condition.

In a diary you can note familiar everyday situations and how they go. This helps to notice gradual changes and discuss them in class. Lasting improvements that recur on different days are especially valuable.

Benefit the person can notice

Quality of life is an important part of assessing breathing training. Research helps to make this side of the result visible, and individual work makes it possible to connect it with the person’s real goals. The aim of the practice is the normalization of breathing, which becomes part of a fuller everyday life.
