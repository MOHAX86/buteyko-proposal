The Bohr effect in modern physiology

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. The Bohr effect is a change in hemoglobin’s ability to hold oxygen: a rise in CO₂ and acidity makes it easier to release oxygen to the tissues.

The Bohr effect explains how hemoglobin changes its ability to hold oxygen depending on the surrounding conditions. CO₂ and the acidity of the environment play an important role in this.

This mechanism helps the body deliver oxygen to where it is used.

How oxygen gets into the tissues

In the lungs, hemoglobin binds oxygen, and then the blood carries it to the organs. In the tissues, conditions change: cells consume oxygen and form metabolic products, including CO₂.

A rise in CO₂ and the associated change in acidity help to reduce hemoglobin’s affinity for oxygen. This makes it easier to release oxygen to the tissues. The point of the mechanism is seen especially clearly during muscular work, when metabolism becomes more active.

Why the gas balance matters

Oxygen supply depends on several links: the intake of air into the lungs, blood flow, the amount of hemoglobin and the conditions of oxygen release. The Bohr effect shows the importance of this last link in particular.

When people speak of the benefit of a normal CO₂ level, this includes preserving the conditions for this natural mechanism. In the Buteyko school, this understanding helps to explain the aim of working with excess ventilation.

The point of the practice is to bring breathing to the physiological norm. Relaxation and attention to factors that deepen breathing are viewed as ways of moving toward this aim.

What modern science studies

Modern research continues to refine our understanding of how CO₂, acidity, temperature and the properties of hemoglobin interact. Laboratory experiments measure how oxygen binding changes under different conditions.

These data develop the classic concept: oxygen release is regulated by the properties of the blood and the state of the tissues.

How this helps to understand the method

For a person studying Buteyko, this is an important part of theoretical preparation. Understanding the mechanism helps to take a thoughtful attitude to the normalization of breathing and to see the connection between the respiratory gases, the blood and the work of the tissues. The practical exercises, meanwhile, are arranged according to the student’s condition, together with an instructor.
