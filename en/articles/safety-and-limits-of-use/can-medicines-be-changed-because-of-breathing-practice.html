Can medicines be changed because of breathing practice

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Not on your own. Even if you feel better, stopping medicines and changing doses are discussed with your treating doctor, and the doctor makes the decision.

If you feel better while practicing, the question of further prescriptions naturally arises. Such changes are worth discussing with your treating doctor. The decision depends on the disease, the medicine, how long the improvement has lasted, and the results of monitoring.

Why a coordinated review matters

Different medicines serve different purposes. Some are used for quick relief of symptoms, others maintain control of the disease when used regularly. The absence of complaints today does not mean that all prescriptions have lost their significance.

That is why you do not stop medicines or reduce doses on your own based on a control pause result. This also applies when a person is sure they are doing the exercises correctly.

What to prepare for the conversation

It is useful to write down the names and doses of your medicines, how they were prescribed, and how you actually use them. Next to this, note changes in symptoms, waking at night, tolerance of everyday activities, and your Buteyko practice.

If you need your symptom relief medicine more often, you should also tell your doctor. Such a record helps to discuss your condition more accurately than one good day or a high pause reading.

How to discuss possible changes

At the appointment, you can ask by what signs disease control is assessed, when a review of treatment is acceptable, and how to monitor your condition after the regimen is changed. It is good if the next steps are clear in advance.

If the doctor has already given you a written plan with conditions for changing treatment, follow that plan. A general article does not replace individual prescriptions.

The role of the instructor

The instructor helps to organize breathing practice with your condition in mind and tells you which observations are useful to keep. Decisions about medicines are made by the treating doctor. If the teacher has medical training, you need to understand whether in a particular situation they are acting specifically as the doctor in charge of treatment.

Feeling better is a reason to have a substantive talk with your doctor. Monitoring of the disease is continued.

When an in-person doctor’s consultation is needed

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
