Breathing exercises with chronic diseases: precautions

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. The program is chosen with the diagnosis, treatment, and the doctor’s restrictions in mind, and it is reviewed during a flare-up or when a new complaint appears.

With a chronic disease, Buteyko practice requires getting to know the person’s condition. The diagnosis, current state of health, treatment, and restrictions are important. Choosing a manageable program starts with this information.

What is clarified before classes

You need to report recent flare-ups, hospital stays, operations, and changes in therapy. Difficulties with ordinary walking, fainting episodes, and recently appeared symptoms also matter.

If a doctor has set restrictions, they are passed on to the instructor. You should not assume that a general recommendation to “do breathing exercises” automatically permits any intensity, body position, or breath holds.

How the practice is chosen

Depending on a person’s abilities, the position, duration, and difficulty of the task are changed. A person may find it more comfortable to work sitting down, take more breaks, or first learn relaxation in detail.

The participant should understand what exactly they are being offered and which sensations are used to judge how it is performed. If an instruction remains unclear, it is better to go through it before repeating it on your own.

What is observed over time

It is useful to note how you feel, your usual symptoms, how well you tolerate everyday activity, and whether you follow your prescriptions. The control pause can complement a diary, but it does not determine the severity of all diseases and does not replace medical examinations.

It makes sense to compare results under similar conditions. Information about sleep, illness, and unusual exertion helps to understand the circumstances in which the readings changed.

If your condition has changed

A flare-up or a new complaint is a reason to review the current program. The question of medical care is decided by how severe the symptoms are and what kind they are. A severe worsening should not be tested with additional exercises.

Long-term work is built on regularity and feedback, with attention to the disease and the necessary treatment.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous.
