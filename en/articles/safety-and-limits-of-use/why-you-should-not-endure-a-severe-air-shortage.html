Why you should not endure a severe air shortage

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. A strong air shortage often causes tension and gets in the way of the main skill: relaxing and reducing the depth of breathing. A light, manageable sensation is enough.

In Buteyko training, a person may become familiar with the sensation of a slight air shortage. Its intensity is chosen individually. The goal is to learn to relax and to reduce excessive depth of breathing while keeping the work controllable and manageable.

Where a manageable sensation ends

A slight sensation and an agonizing need to inhale are experienced differently. If a person tenses the whole body, starts to fear the next breath, or takes a long time to recover after an exercise, the way it is performed needs to be reviewed with the instructor.

The suitable load cannot be determined from someone else’s description alone. The same task may be accompanied by different sensations in different participants. They are influenced by the initial condition, experience, and the specific way the task is performed.

Why tension gets in the way

Trying to suppress the inhalation by force is often accompanied by clenching of the abdomen, chest, face, and shoulders. The person is busy enduring and stops noticing how unnecessary tension arises.

In the school’s methodological position, the role of willpower has to do with organizing the practice: setting aside time, understanding the task, observing yourself, and working with factors that deepen breathing. It is not a requirement to endure suffocation.

The control pause without competition

The control pause is not turned into a maximum breath hold. It is ended before the stage that takes strong willpower; after the measurement, breathing should remain close to how it was before. If gaining seconds takes marked effort, such a result should not be accepted as a quality measurement.

What helps you learn

It is useful to go through in detail with the instructor how the exercise begins and when it ends. Sometimes it is enough to reduce the intensity, shorten the task, or first pay more attention to relaxation.

Marked shortness of breath in the presence of a disease cannot automatically be regarded as a desirable training sensation. If air shortage occurs outside practice or increases noticeably, its causes are discussed with a doctor.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous.
