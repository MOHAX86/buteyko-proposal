Breathing practice during pregnancy: precautions

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Sessions are chosen with regard to the stage of pregnancy, how the woman feels and the obstetrician’s advice, without severe air shortage or maximal breath holds. In case of feeling faint or unwell, dizziness or pain, the exercise is stopped.

During pregnancy, the program of sessions is chosen with regard to its stage and course, how the woman feels and the obstetrician’s recommendations. If the woman is already familiar with the Buteyko method, it is useful to tell the doctor about this. Starting new training should also be discussed in advance.

What is important before a session

The instructor needs to know about the pregnancy, medical restrictions and unusual symptoms. Previous experience does not mean that the load should remain unchanged throughout the whole pregnancy.

It is useful to clarify which position is comfortable, how long the woman can practice without fatigue and which tasks raise questions.

Content of the practice

The school’s general principles (relaxation, gradualness and reducing excessive depth of breathing to the norm) do not mean that a severe air shortage must be achieved. Pregnancy is not a reason to test maximal breath holds or to compete over the control pause.

Exercises with breath holds and noticeable restriction of breathing require separate approval. Without an individual assessment, it is not possible to recommend the same number of seconds, number of repetitions or target readings for all pregnant women.

How to take into account how you feel

The body position is changed if it causes discomfort. In case of feeling faint or unwell, dizziness, pain or increasing shortness of breath, the exercise is stopped. It is better to describe doubtful sensations to a specialist than to try to check them yourself with one more repetition.

Sudden or severe shortness of breath, especially together with chest pain, fainting or bleeding, requires urgent medical help. Such symptoms are not considered part of the ordinary mastering of a breathing skill.

Consistent support

Recommendations are refined as the condition changes. Promises of special results for the child or for the course of labor must not take the place of individual obstetric monitoring.

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous. Results are not guaranteed.
