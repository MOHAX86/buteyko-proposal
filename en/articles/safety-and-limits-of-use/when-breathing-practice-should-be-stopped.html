When breathing practice should be stopped

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. The exercise is stopped in case of marked air shortage, dizziness, darkening of vision, or pain or heaviness in the chest. Dangerous signs require emergency care.

Knowing how to stop an exercise in time is part of competent practice. A break is especially important if tension and breathing discomfort grow instead of relaxation.

When a break is needed

The exercise is stopped if marked air shortage, dizziness, darkening of vision, pain, or unusual heaviness in the chest appears. You should not continue the task for the sake of the planned time, an impressive control pause, or a wish to do it “correctly”.

After stopping, take a stable, comfortable position and let your breathing recover without trying to forcibly make it shallower or hold it. If your condition requires care prescribed by a doctor, follow your own medical plan.

What to tell the instructor

For the review, it is important to describe what exactly was done, how long the exercise lasted, when the sensations began, and how they changed after stopping. It is useful to mention lack of sleep, a recent illness, food intake, and medicines if this is relevant to the situation.

A specific description helps to check the technique and decide whether a medical consultation is needed.

When urgent help is required

Severe difficulty breathing, inability to speak in normal sentences, loss of consciousness, blue lips, marked chest pain, or sudden confusion require emergency medical care. In such a situation, do not wait for the instructor’s reply and do not try to correct the condition with a training breath hold.

How to return to classes

If the sensations were mild and have passed completely, discuss how the task was performed before repeating it. With recurring or unusual symptoms, the cause is found out first. The return to practice is built around a clear correction, not an attempt to withstand the previous load at any cost.

When an in-person doctor’s consultation is needed

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous.
