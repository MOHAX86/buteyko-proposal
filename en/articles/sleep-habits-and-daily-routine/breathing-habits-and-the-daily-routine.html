Breathing habits and the daily routine

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. A daily routine helps to make practice stable, but it does not serve as a strict system of breathing control. A few comfortable habits and attention to symptoms are more useful than trying to spend every day perfectly.

The routine sets the context

The time you get up, work, meals, movement and evening rest form an ordinary day. Breathing habits arise within this rhythm: one person rushes in the morning and notices sharp breaths, another works late and cannot relax for a long time. The Buteyko method does not require devoting the whole day to controlling breaths. It is more useful to choose recurring situations for calm observation.

How to build in a short observation

For example, after a work call you notice tense shoulders and take a pause to sit comfortably. During a walk you slow down if you have started keeping up nasal breathing by force. Such decisions are tied to habitual actions and do not require the same breathing at rest and during exertion. The detailed technique of practice and its duration are determined with an instructor.

What helps night rest

A relatively constant sleep time, daytime activity and a calm evening support healthy sleep habits. Late caffeine or heavy food may disturb some people. If the routine keeps shifting because of shift work or caring for family members, the recommendations need to be adapted to reality; there is no point in blaming yourself for “wrong breathing.”

Do not look for a single cause

A dry mouth in the morning may be connected with a blocked nose and other causes, and sleepiness with lack of sleep or illness. Notes about breathing alone do not reveal a diagnosis. Snoring with pauses in breathing, waking up choking and marked sleepiness require a consultation with a doctor. Notes about your routine will help to describe the circumstances more precisely.

How to avoid overload

Choose one change for the coming days: a comfortable evening ritual, a habitual time to get up or a short observation of tension after work. Do not change sleep, diet and the whole practice at the same time, otherwise it is hard to understand what happened. If the sessions cause worsening, reduce the load and discuss it with an instructor.

If your schedule changes

A shift worker may find it hard to keep to the same bedtime. In that case it is more practical to rely on repeatable elements: calm time before sleep, a comfortable place for the session and a short note about how you feel. Rigidly copying someone else’s schedule does not help. It is important not to cut necessary sleep for the sake of doing an exercise. If work causes persistent sleepiness or sleep disorders, discuss this with a doctor. An instructor will help to revise the individual practice so that it fits into the real schedule.
