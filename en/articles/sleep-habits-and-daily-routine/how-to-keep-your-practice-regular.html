How to keep your practice regular

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Stable practice is supported by a manageable schedule, a convenient moment of the day and adjustment according to your condition. There is no need to make up for what you missed with a double volume.

Regularity is not the same as perfection

Practice becomes more stable when it fits into an ordinary day and does not require special conditions every time. Missing a session does not make the previous work useless. In the Buteyko school, the finer points of performance are corrected by an instructor, so regularity means repeating a suitable, agreed practice, not trying to increase the time or endure more every day.

Tie the session to a clear moment

Choose a convenient time: after getting up in the morning, in the middle of the day or before a calm evening. One recognizable cue in your schedule that reminds you of the session is enough. At the same time, do not cut sleep for the sake of obligatory practice and do not start an exercise right after a heavy meal if that is uncomfortable. The routine should support your well-being, not create a new source of haste.

Watch the quality, not just the streak of days

Fifteen marks in a calendar do not tell how a person performed the exercise. It is more important whether symptoms are getting worse and whether a severe air shortage is appearing. The methodological framework of the school takes into account how you feel, symptoms and the trend of the control pause under comparable conditions. If the session has become mechanical and tense, discuss it with an instructor.

What to do on an inconvenient day

In case of lack of sleep, illness or severe fatigue, make it easier or take a break if that is safer. Do not make up for a missed session with a double volume the next day. New or unusual symptoms call for a medical assessment. Flexibility helps to keep the practice going for months better than a rigid goal at any cost.

Leave room for feedback

Briefly write down what was comfortable and what caused difficulty; bring specific questions to the session. If your routine has changed because of work or family, you can revise the volume together with your instructor. In this way VEDB remains a consciously organized practice, not a continuous struggle with your own breathing.

When the schedule did not work

If a long evening session regularly falls through because you come home late, revise the time and volume. Perhaps another moment of the day is more convenient. The schedule should match real life; this helps you come back to practice after missed sessions.
