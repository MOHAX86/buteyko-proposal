Eating and breathing practice

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. If it is uncomfortable to practice after a heavy meal, it is better to postpone the session or make it easier. There is no single interval for everyone; do not practice through marked discomfort, and if symptoms recur, see a doctor.

Comfort changes after eating

Right after a heavy meal, bending, a strained posture and a complex breathing exercise may feel uncomfortable. This is a reason to postpone the session or make it easier, not to force yourself to fulfill the plan at any cost. In the methodological framework of the school, nutrition is included in the list of factors that can influence breathing habits; eating in itself is not declared a cause of illness.

Do not prescribe universal minutes

People differ in the amount of food they eat, how they feel and what illnesses they have. So there is no pause after eating that is the same for everyone. If your instructor has given you an individual schedule, follow it and report any discomfort. Heaviness, heartburn or pain after eating are not a “breathing mistake” by default; if these symptoms recur, a doctor’s consultation is needed.

The evening meal and sleep

General healthy sleep recommendations advise avoiding very heavy or large meals immediately before bedtime. This is not a requirement of the Buteyko method and not a ban on any evening snack. If a late dinner keeps you from sleeping, try changing the time or amount together with your daily routine. Do not replace dietary and medical decisions with breathing practice.

How to organize a session

Choose a time when it is comfortable to sit, you do not have to tense your face and belly, and your attention is not taken up by a feeling of fullness. If you want to rest right now, take a break. Do not hold your belly in by force and do not do breath holds to the limit. In the school, the correctness of practice is assessed by how you feel and the trend of symptoms; marked worsening requires the load to be reviewed.

Particular medical situations

With diseases of the gastrointestinal tract, pregnancy, diabetes and a need to take medicines with food, the routine may be individual. Do not change prescribed diet or treatment because of a general text about breathing. Discuss how to combine sessions and meals with your attending doctor and your instructor, each within their competence.

An example of an ordinary evening

If after a heavy dinner it is uncomfortable to sit or your attention keeps being drawn to the fullness of your stomach, it is better to postpone the session. At the same time, you should not push sleep ever later to fulfill the plan. Recurring discomfort after eating that also arises outside practice requires separate investigation.

Can you practice on an empty stomach?

There is no single rule for everyone. How you feel and your individual medical circumstances are what matter. A person who feels unwell without food should not adjust to someone else’s pattern. Choose the time with your instructor, without changing your prescribed diet.
