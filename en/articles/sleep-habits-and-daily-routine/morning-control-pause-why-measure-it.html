Morning control pause: why measure it

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. The morning control pause (MCP) is measured right after waking up in order to observe the trend under similar conditions. The pause is ended at the first desire to inhale and is assessed together with how you feel; it is not a diagnosis.

What MCP means

MCP is the morning control pause. In the school it is measured right after waking up, while still in bed, before getting up and before any morning activities. After a calm ordinary exhalation, you note the time until the first desire to inhale, without strained endurance. It is important that after the pause your breathing stays about the same as before it. This is an indicator for observation within the method, not an independent analysis of alveolar CO₂ or a diagnosis.

Why choose the morning

A morning measurement helps to compare results under similar conditions. In the methodological framework of the school, the morning value is used to observe the trend. But a single figure depends on sleep, the condition of the nose, illness, stress and other circumstances.

How not to distort the result

Having woken up, sit comfortably on the bed without standing up or starting your morning activities. Relax your belly and shoulders. After an ordinary calm exhalation, note the time until the first desire to inhale. Do not try to hold out as long as possible. If after the pause you have to inhale sharply or take several deep breaths, the measurement was excessive and should not be considered a successful control pause.

How to read your records

It is better to look at a series of comparable measurements together with how you feel and your symptoms, rather than judge yourself by a single morning. A low value after lack of sleep does not prove the cause of a bad night; a high one does not rule out sleep apnea. An instructor helps to discuss the trend and the measurement conditions. If your sleep is disturbed or family members observe breathing stops, see a doctor regardless of your MCP.

Why you should not repeat the measurement many times

Do not repeat the measurement many times while sitting on the bed: the breath holds may turn into a competition and increase tension. It is enough to follow the agreed procedure and write down the result without trying to improve it with each next attempt. The morning serves as a point of observation, not an exam. If your condition worsens, the measurement is stopped and the practice is discussed with a specialist.

The morning after an unusual night

If you went to bed late, fell ill or woke up because of noise, the morning measurement may differ from the usual one. Do not try to “fix” the figure with several extra breath holds. Make a short note about the circumstances and compare the data when conditions become similar again. The school uses several criteria of correct practice, including how you feel and your symptoms. A single MCP figure should not determine your mood for the whole day.
