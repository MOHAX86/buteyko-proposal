Breathing during sleep and the Buteyko method

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. In the Buteyko school, daytime practice is considered the basis for a calmer night’s rest, but it does not guarantee the same breathing all night. In case of loud snoring, pauses in breathing during sleep, waking up choking or marked daytime sleepiness, discuss an examination with a doctor.

What changes at night

During sleep, a person does not consciously control every breath. Breathing changes with the sleep stages, body position and the condition of the upper airways. If you manage to relax and breathe more calmly during the day, it cannot automatically be assumed that your breathing will be the same all night. That is why it is important to distinguish between daytime practice, night habits and medical sleep disorders.

The position of the Buteyko school

In the Buteyko school, the goal of practice is to normalize respiratory gas values, primarily CO₂, and to reduce excessive depth of breathing to the physiological norm, mainly through relaxation. Regular daytime work is regarded as the basis for a calmer night’s rest. The evening session is chosen individually: it may be light movement or relaxation. This approach does not mean a promise to cure snoring, insomnia or apnea.

What you can notice at home

Pay attention to whether it is easy to breathe through your nose before sleep, whether you wake up with a dry mouth, and how refreshed you feel in the morning. It is useful to note sleep times, night awakenings, caffeine in the evening and changes in physical load. Such notes will help in a conversation with an instructor and a doctor if complaints appear.

An important safety limit

Loud snoring, pauses in breathing during sleep noticed by family members, waking up choking and marked daytime sleepiness are reasons to discuss an examination with a doctor. Sleep apnea cannot be diagnosed by the morning control pause or corrected by forcibly keeping the mouth closed. Do not stop prescribed therapy and do not tape your mouth at night on the advice of an article.

The night routine matters too

A habitual sleep time, a calm hour before going to bed, a cool and quiet room and moderate caffeine intake are ordinary parts of caring for sleep. They are not a separate Buteyko technique, but they affect the conditions in which a person practices. If sleep is short because of your schedule, a breathing exercise does not replace the necessary hours of rest. If the difficulty persists, look for the cause together with a specialist.

One evening, different explanations

Two people may wake up equally tired for different reasons. One went to bed late and slept little; the other spent enough time in bed, but family members noticed snoring and breathing stops. In the first case the routine needs to be reviewed, in the second the sleep needs to be examined. Notes about the circumstances of the night help to tell such situations apart.

Result

Work with breathing includes a mastered daytime practice and a suitable evening session. At the same time, the routine and the quality of night rest are taken into account. If there are signs of disordered breathing during sleep, a separate medical assessment is required.
