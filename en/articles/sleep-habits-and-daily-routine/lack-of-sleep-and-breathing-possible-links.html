Lack of sleep and breathing: possible links

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Lack of sleep and breathing may together affect how you feel, but the cause needs to be checked. After a bad night choose rest and light practice, and in case of recurring sleepiness, snoring and pauses in breathing, see a doctor.

When sleep is short

After a short night, a person may find it harder to concentrate, and ordinary tasks feel heavier. The subjective perception of breathing may also change: a familiar walk or conversation requires more attention. These sensations cannot establish what exactly has changed in the blood gases or the alveoli. Nor should lack of sleep automatically be explained by “wrong breathing.”

Several possible links

Stress can both interfere with falling asleep and make breaths more noticeable. Illness can disturb sleep and make it harder to breathe through the nose. Snoring with pauses in breathing, on the other hand, points to possible disordered breathing during sleep, which affects the quality of rest. In each situation the cause and direction of the link differ; there is no single universal explanation.

What to do after a sleepless night

Do not try to get your strength back with a hard breathing exercise. If you are tired, make the practice easier and give priority to ordinary recovery and routine. In the methodological position of the school, forcibly reducing breathing, a strong air shortage and continuing an exercise when your condition worsens are considered mistakes. Discuss with your instructor how to change the load on such a day.

When sleep needs to be assessed

If you regularly sleep long enough but remain extremely sleepy during the day, wake up with a choking sensation, or family members notice breathing stops, see a doctor. These are possible signs of apnea and other conditions. The cause is established by examination, not by the control pause.

What to write down

For several days, note sleep times, awakenings, caffeine in the evening, the condition of your nose and how you feel in the morning. Do not turn the diary into strict control of every minute: it is needed to see recurring patterns and prepare specific questions for a specialist. Discuss chronic lack of sleep as a matter of routine or health, not only as an obstacle to a breathing exercise.

Short sleep and the next day’s load

After lack of sleep, habitual activity may feel harder. Make the load lighter and leave time for recovery. One bad night does not allow you to judge a lasting change in breathing; if the problem recurs, notes about your routine will help to describe it more precisely to a specialist.

When an in-person doctor’s consultation is needed

This material is for information and does not replace a doctor’s consultation and treatment. Do not cancel or change medicines and their doses on your own. Chest pain, pronounced difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness require emergency help. Breath holds must not be performed in water, while driving or where fainting is dangerous.
