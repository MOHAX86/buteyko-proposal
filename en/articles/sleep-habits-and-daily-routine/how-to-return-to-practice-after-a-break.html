How to return to practice after a break

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Start from how you feel now, not from your former record, and do not try to catch up on what you missed. Check the volume with your instructor, and after an illness take the doctor’s recommendations into account.

A break does not erase all your experience

After an illness, a trip or a stressful period, the usual practice routine may be disrupted. This is no reason to “catch up” on missed sessions with long breath holds or excessive load. First assess your current condition: how you sleep, whether there are any symptoms, whether it is comfortable to sit and breathe through your nose. A familiar exercise may feel different from how it felt before the break.

Go back to the simple

Begin with calm observation of your breathing and bodily comfort under ordinary conditions. If your instructor prescribed a sequence of sessions, contact them to clarify what volume to resume practice with. Do not automatically carry the former duration over to the first day after an illness. The goal of the method is to bring excessive ventilation down to normal through relaxation, not to fulfill an accumulated plan at any cost.

Assess the response

After a session, note your general condition, symptoms and any marked discomfort. In the methodological framework of the school, worsening is considered a sign of a possible mistake or excessive load. Do not turn the control pause into an exam and do not push for your former number of seconds on the very first day. For a comparable trend, the measurement is taken calmly, under similar conditions.

If the break was due to illness

If symptoms persist, especially shortness of breath, chest pain, dizziness and marked weakness, first discuss a return to physical and breathing load with a doctor. Do not explain the illness solely by the break in exercises. Prescribed treatment is continued according to the medical plan, and the instructor helps to adapt the methodological part.

Create a stable rhythm

Choose a manageable volume and a convenient time. In the first days it is more useful to restore a clear order of actions than to return immediately to the former duration.

An example of returning after a trip

After a week on the road, first restore your usual routine and assess how you feel. Then check the suitable volume of sessions. If the trip involved an illness, take into account any persisting symptoms and medical restrictions.
