History of VEDB: main stages of development

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. VEDB went from a doctor’s observations in 1952 to research in Novosibirsk, clinical trials, the 1985 order of the USSR Ministry of Health, and the training of teachers.

VEDB (Voluntary Elimination of Deep Breathing) is the historical name of the Buteyko method. Behind this abbreviation lies a step-by-step development: from the first medical observations to research work, clinical application, and the training of teachers.

Each stage added its own part to the method. Observations raised questions, measurements helped investigate breathing, and work with students turned knowledge into a practical skill.

Observation and the formation of the idea

The initial stage is connected with Buteyko’s medical studies and the events of 1952. His attention was drawn to the features of increased breathing and changes in well-being. The idea of the importance of excess ventilation and the respiratory gases gradually took shape.

An important direction of further inquiry was the relationship between external respiration and the body’s internal needs. The practical work developed around normalizing this relationship.

Research into breathing

The next major stage is connected with Novosibirsk. Memoirs of that time describe laboratory research, instruments, observation of patients, and discussions of the results.

Essential to the development of the method was the effort to link visible changes in breathing with physiological indicators. Practical experience went hand in hand with a search for ways to measure and explain what was happening.

Clinical application

In 1968, a clinical trial took place in Leningrad. In 1981, a trial in children with bronchial asthma was conducted at the First Moscow Medical Institute. These events became notable milestones in the professional discussion of the method.

In 1983, an inventor’s certificate was issued for a method of treating hemohypocarbia. In 1985, the USSR Ministry of Health issued an order on measures to introduce the method in the treatment of bronchial asthma and to study it further.

Formation of the school

Spreading the method required teachers. Books about Buteyko pay much attention to how classes were held, how the theory was explained, how performance was checked, and how everyday practice was discussed.

By the end of the Soviet period, seminars, conferences, and the work of instructors from different cities were developing. Notes, lectures, and teaching materials were passed on along with personal teaching experience.

How to understand voluntary effort

In the school of Alik Mullakhmetov’s Health Academy, the voluntary component means consciously organized work on normalizing breathing: setting aside time, mastering relaxation, completing assignments, and noticing factors that deepen breathing.

Excessive depth of inhalation is reduced mainly through relaxation. Theoretical understanding becomes useful thanks to correctly mastered practice.
