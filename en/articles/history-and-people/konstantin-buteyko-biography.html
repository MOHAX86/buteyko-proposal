Konstantin Buteyko: biography

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. Konstantin Pavlovich Buteyko (1923–2003) was a doctor who devoted his professional life to studying breathing and creating a method for normalizing it.

In his work, Konstantin Pavlovich Buteyko combined medical observation, an interest in physiology, and a desire to teach people to take part in improving their own condition.

Behind the name known today lies a path several decades long: medical education, first observations, laboratory research, clinical trials, and the training of students. This explains why attentiveness, consistency, and practical training are so important in his school.

The beginning of the path

Buteyko was born on January 27, 1923, in the village of Ivanytsia, in the territory of present-day Ukraine. His youth fell in the war years. Later, medicine became the main work of his life: in 1946 he began studying at the First Moscow Medical Institute, and in 1952 he graduated from it with honors.

While still a student, he paid attention to how patients breathed and how their condition changed. Later, in a university lecture, Buteyko recalled an incident during a class in internal medicine: after prolonged increased breathing while his lungs were being listened to, a patient felt unwell. This episode prompted him to look for an explanation of what was happening.

From a medical question to a method

The key turning point in the history of the method dates to October 1952. Buteyko linked excessive depth of breathing with a change in well-being and began to study this connection systematically. Lung ventilation and the role of carbon dioxide were at the center of his attention.

The work continued in Novosibirsk. Research, laboratory measurements, observation of patients, and the formation of a practical approach are connected with this period. The method gradually became a system that had to be explained, mastered, and passed on to other specialists.

Researcher and teacher

Buteyko spoke to doctors and to wider audiences, trained followers, and took part in discussing the results of applying the method. Important historical milestones include the clinical trials, the 1983 inventor’s certificate, and the 1985 order of the USSR Ministry of Health on introducing the method in the treatment of asthma.

A large part of this work was training people capable of teaching the practice. Students’ memoirs preserve accounts of the classes, the analysis of mistakes, and a demanding attitude toward understanding the method.

Legacy

Konstantin Buteyko passed away in 2003. His ideas continued to be studied and taught in various countries. Lectures, books, documents, and the recollections of people who worked alongside him have been preserved.

At Alik Mullakhmetov’s Health Academy, the goal of the method is formulated as the normalization of breathing and gas indicators. The main attention is given to relaxation, to reducing excessive depth of inhalation to the physiological norm, and to work with factors that deepen breathing.
