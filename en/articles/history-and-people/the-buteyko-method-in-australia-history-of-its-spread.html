The Buteyko method in Australia: history of its spread

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. In Australia, acquaintance with the Buteyko method began in 1990 with the arrival of Alexander Stalmatski. Later, local teachers, their association, and a clinical study in asthma appeared there.

Australia became one of the important centers of the international spread of the Buteyko method. Early English-language courses, the training of local teachers, and a notable clinical study in asthma are connected with the Australian branch.

The beginning of teaching

In 1990, Alexander Stalmatski came to Australia and began introducing people to the method. He went on to hold courses and train specialists who could teach independently.

Students were able to continue their work in their own language environment, ask for clarification, and, after appropriate training, pass their experience on to others.

The courses gradually formed a circle of teachers and participants. Personal experience of the classes became an occasion for new meetings, discussions, and wider acquaintance with Buteyko’s approach.

The emergence of an association

In 1996, a group of trained teachers founded the Buteyko Institute of Breathing and Health. Its development is connected with organizing training, professional contacts, and interest in researching the method.

The association helped specialists stay in touch. They could discuss the content of classes, share observations, and develop teacher training.

Clinical study

An important milestone was a study carried out in Brisbane in the 1990s. Its results were published in the Medical Journal of Australia in 1998.

The study assessed breathing training in people with asthma. It drew professional attention to changes in symptoms and in the use of medicines. For the international history, the publication of the results in a medical journal and the opportunity for further scientific discussion were significant.

From local classes to international ties

Australian teachers took part in exchanging experience with followers of the method in other countries. Contacts, conferences, and specialist training developed around the teaching.

The history of this branch clearly shows the importance of continuity: first mastering the method, then training teachers and creating conditions for further work.

What matters for today’s reader

The Australian experience helps us see the path from first acquaintance with an idea to organized practice. Clear explanation, the opportunity to study with a teacher, and sustained interest in the results of training proved important for its spread.

These tasks remain relevant in any country. The method becomes more accessible when a person can receive good theoretical preparation, master practical skills, and continue working with feedback.
