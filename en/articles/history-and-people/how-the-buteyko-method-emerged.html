How the Buteyko method emerged

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. It all began with the student Buteyko’s observations of how patients breathed, and the turning point came in the fall of 1952. Long work then turned the idea into a system of training.

The history of the Buteyko method began with a medical question: how are the features of breathing and a person’s condition connected? The future creator of the method noticed this connection while still in medical training and later made it the subject of many years of work.

It was important to him to understand why the usual advice to breathe harder was in some circumstances accompanied by a worsening of well-being. The search for an answer led to the study of excess ventilation and the role of CO₂.

The question arose at a patient’s bedside

In a 1972 lecture, Buteyko spoke about his practical training in the third year of medical school. While his lungs were being listened to, a patient breathed deeply for a long time at the student’s request and lost consciousness. His condition recovered, but what had happened remained an important question for the young researcher.

He began to watch patients’ breathing more attentively and to look for a physiological explanation: how breathing is involved in the state of the body and what changes when ventilation increases.

The turning point in the fall of 1952

In the history of the school, the date the method originated is considered to be October 7 (the night of October 6 to 7), 1952. Buteyko’s account of a change in his own well-being when he reduced excessive depth of breathing is connected with this period.

He then began to test his observations in work with patients. An idea gradually took shape that it is important for the body that breathing correspond to the current needs of metabolism. Carbon dioxide and the consequences of its excessive removal took a central place in the explanation.

How the system of training emerged

A great deal of work lies between the original idea and the practical method. It was necessary to determine what to teach a person, how to observe the trend, how to distinguish correct performance from overstrain, and how to organize sessions in ordinary life.

Later, the training came to include relaxation, attention to excessive depth of inhalation, and work with the circumstances that increase breathing. Methodological guidelines, observation diaries, and teacher training appeared.

Memoirs about Buteyko’s work show this transition clearly: from searching for an explanation to a detailed examination of how a student sits, relaxes, performs an assignment, and feels afterward.

The main task of the method

In the current practical understanding of the school of Alik Mullakhmetov’s Health Academy, the task is to normalize breathing to the physiological norm. Excessive depth of inhalation is reduced mainly through relaxation.

Buteyko sought a way to work with a person’s breathing behavior itself. His idea was developed in a system of training in which understanding, regular practice, and feedback from an instructor are important.
