Development of the Buteyko method in the USSR

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. In the USSR, the method went from the work of a single doctor to clinical trials, the 1985 order of the Ministry of Health on its introduction for bronchial asthma, and the formation of a circle of teachers.

In the Soviet Union, the Buteyko method developed in medical institutions and the scientific community, at lectures and practical classes.

The Novosibirsk period

Important years of Buteyko’s research are connected with Novosibirsk. Materials about this period describe a laboratory, measurements of breathing indicators, discussions with colleagues, and observation of patients.

The task was to study breathing as a regulated physiological process and to develop ways of normalizing it. This required work with instruments, clinical attentiveness, and the steady accumulation of experience.

S. G. Altukhov’s books preserve many episodes of this life. They show the intense work, Buteyko’s persistence, and the importance of the people who helped continue the research and teaching.

Clinical trials

One of the main milestones was the 1968 trial at the All-Union Research Institute of Pulmonology in Leningrad. The conclusion describes a therapeutic effect in asthma and a decrease in the number and severity of attacks.

In 1981, the method was studied in children with bronchial asthma at the I. M. Sechenov First Moscow Medical Institute. The document on the results of this work noted its effectiveness as part of combined treatment.

In this way, the practice became a subject of organized clinical observation and professional discussion.

Documents and further work

In 1983, Buteyko received an inventor’s certificate. Then, on April 30, 1985, the USSR Ministry of Health issued an order on measures to introduce the method of voluntary regulation of the depth of breathing in the treatment of bronchial asthma.

The document provided for further research and the preparation of methodological materials. It became a notable administrative milestone in the history of the method.

Passing on experience

In the following years, the importance of teaching grew. Lectures explained the theoretical principles, while classes helped students master relaxation, observation of breathing, and regular practice.

In the second half of the 1980s, meetings and conferences were held where instructors from different cities exchanged experience. Memoirs preserve a description of the conference in Yevpatoria in the fall of 1988.

By the end of the Soviet period, a community of students and teachers existed around the method. It was this community that helped preserve the materials, continue the teaching, and pass the accumulated experience on to the next generation.
