The spread of the Buteyko method abroad

Author: Alik Mullakhmetov, VEDB senior instructor and examiner, more than 30 years of practicing the Buteyko method

Updated: November 25, 2026

In short. The method spread abroad through people who mastered it and began to teach; Australia and the United Kingdom became especially prominent directions.

The Buteyko method came to other countries with practical courses. Books, professional associations, and clinical studies were then added to them.

Australia and the United Kingdom became especially prominent directions. Their history helps us see how an approach that arose in the Soviet medical environment became part of the international discussion of breathing training.

The role of teachers

In 1990, Alexander Stalmatski introduced the method to an Australian audience. He held classes and trained teachers. Thanks to this, the training gradually went beyond individual visits and gained a local continuation.

This way of spreading was of great importance. The practical skill was passed on through explanation, observation of performance, and the student’s subsequent work. The appearance of local specialists made it possible to sustain this process.

Interest from the professional community

The next step was meetings with medical specialists and research programs. In the late 1990s, the results of an Australian randomized study in asthma were published.

Scientific publications made it possible to discuss the method in the language of specific indicators: symptoms, use of medicines, and quality of life. In the history of its spread, the very possibility of such a professional dialogue is important.

Associations and exchange of experience

Communities of teachers formed in various countries. They helped organize training, exchange materials, and support professional development.

International meetings gave an opportunity to compare teaching experience. In 2000, a conference of followers of the method took place in New Zealand. Participants could discuss training and meet people from other schools and countries.

What changes in the move to another language environment

Translating the method requires precision. The meaning of excess ventilation, normalization of breathing, relaxation, and the control pause must be conveyed. What the student actually learns depends on the quality of the explanation.

In different countries, training, research, and the exchange of experience developed around Buteyko’s approach, while a person’s work on their own breathing skill remained at the center.
